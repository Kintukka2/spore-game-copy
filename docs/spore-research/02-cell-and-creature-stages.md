# 2. Gameplay Mechanics: Cell Stage and Creature Stage

> Scope: the base game *Spore* (Maxis/EA, 2008), PC/Mac. Where a mechanic or part comes from the *Creepy & Cute* parts pack or the Patch 1.06 "Bot Parts" promotion, it is flagged. Figures come from the official manual, the Spore Wiki (Fandom), StrategyWiki, a GameFAQs-derived FAQ (Feng Kevin Gu, 2008, hosted by GameRevolution), and Speed Demos Archive runner notes. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is stated.

---

## 2.1 CELL STAGE

### 2.1.1 Origin and premise
- The game opens with a cinematic: a comet/meteor falls into a solar system and heads toward the player's planet. It breaks up in the atmosphere and the fragments fall into the ocean. The camera follows one fragment underwater until it cracks open and the player's cell swims out. The manual says you start "hitching a ride to your newly named planet on the tail of a meteor."
- This illustrates **panspermia**, the hypothesis that life is seeded by microorganisms carried on meteors or comets. Wikipedia and the Spore Wiki both describe the opening this way.
- The camera looks top-down on a single 2D plane inside a 3D "tide pool" or "primordial ooze." Wikipedia compares it to *flOw*, and StrategyWiki compares it to *Pac-Man*. The cell deals with currents, predators and food. Its goal is to "eat or be eaten," collect parts and eventually move onto land.
- It is the shortest stage, about **5–15 minutes** depending on difficulty (Spore Wiki). The *Speedfreak* achievement requires finishing it in under 8 minutes. A Speed Demos Archive run finished it in **3:22 on Easy**.
- In-game time runs very fast. The Spore Wiki says the stage covers roughly **2–3 billion in-game years** if finished in 15–20 minutes, about 2.5 million years per real second. Each later stage runs more slowly.

### 2.1.2 Controls and HUD (official manual)
| Action | Control |
|---|---|
| Move/steer | Point and left- or right-click; hold to swim continuously. WASD/arrow keys also work. |
| Zoom | +/- or mouse wheel |
| Call mate (enter Cell Creator) | Mating Call button above the Progress Bar (Backspace) |
| History/timeline | T (toggles the History panel) |

The HUD shows mission objectives, zoom, options, pause, Sporepedia, My Collection, DNA, the **Progress Bar**, the Call Mate button, History, the **Health meter**, and icons for parts and meteor fragments. The manual advises aiming "where the object is going to be, instead of its current location," because currents push the cell around.

### 2.1.3 Choosing a diet and starting cell
- Before the stage starts, the player picks **herbivore** (Filter Mouth) or **carnivore** (Jaw). The choice is not permanent, because mouths can be swapped in the editor.
- No starting option makes the cell an **omnivore**. A cell becomes one by carrying both mouths, by later adding the **Proboscis**, or by eating a balanced diet.
- One of **15 Maxis starting cells** is chosen at random. **6 are carnivores** (Bawb, Fohlees, Ildeeni, Jowwi, Kkroot, Simnu) and **9 are herbivores** (Dolb, Gaal, Glarg, Loobo, Noog, Oog, Sporoo, Urgah, Yurgh).
- Some starting herbivores (the wiki names Dolb, Sporoo and Loobo) have an **extra eye** that can be sold for **5 DNA**. This is a known speed and hard-mode trick.
- The Sporepedia holds **42 Maxis-made cells** in total: 15 starting cells and 27 NPC cells. These are the only cells that ever appear in the Cell Stage, apart from the player's own species. Player-made cells from the Sporepedia do **not** populate the tide pool.

### 2.1.4 Progression, growth and the layered depth/zoom
- The Progress Bar fills as the cell eats. The wiki says there are "roughly **10 levels** of development," but only **five** are marked by vertical dividers on the bar. StrategyWiki describes this as **5 main stages, each split into 2 sub-stages**, and each sub-stage is a growth spurt.
- **Layered world.** Larger cells swim in blurred background layers. As the player grows, the camera zooms out and those layers are "drawn into the foreground" (Wikipedia). Cells that were "peer" size become "prey" size, and "predator" cells move in from the background. Cells that were once huge eventually shrink below the player and disappear.
- The player's cell becomes **less transparent** as it grows and turns fully **opaque at level 4**. When the sea floor becomes visible, the cell is close to evolving. In the final level the water turns a murky orange, like a beach.

| Level | New features | Cells present (wiki) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eat-5-food mission. In Easy/Medium a scripted Chomper kills a Pokey at about level 1.5 and drops the **Spike**; this does not happen on Hard. | Minno, Goldy (Hard only at 1.0, as a predator), Pokey, Chomper |
| 2 | Large green plant flakes appear (2.5). **Jet** (Booster) and **Poison** (Squirty) appear. First **Epic** cell. | Chomper, Shyster, Spiked Shyster, Booster; then Grubby, Squirty, Epic Stabber |
| 3 | **Cilia** (Pinky, Punky) and the **Proboscis** (Nosey). Meteors may contain Poison. | Pinky, Punky, Nosey, Stabber, Stabbella, Ducky, Epic Ducky/Jawhead |
| 4 | **Electric** (Buzzy, Needle Buzzy). Seaweed appears. The cell becomes opaque. | Nosey, Stabbella, Ducky, Buzzy, Jawhead, Squiggly, Needle Buzzy, Puffish, Epic Puffish/Megamouth |
| 5 | Maa lays eggs that hatch into **Juniors**. More meteor bits. No larger background cells. | Jawhead, Squiggly, Puffish, Junior, Jetster, Maa, Paa, Megamouth, Bloato, Epic Snorf |

### 2.1.5 DNA points in the Cell Stage
- Eating food gives DNA points. The wiki says each piece of food from certain sources has "a chance to reward 1 point," shown by a green number. Eating also restores health.
- DNA is both the editor currency and the progress meter. The manual says "DNA points also count towards evolution." Spending DNA on parts does not lower progress.
- **The DNA cap is disputed.** The Spore Wiki's DNA Points page says that when the bar fills, DNA stops being earned and is "capped out" at **125** total, counting DNA already spent on parts. A Cell Stage page on the same wiki says "to a maximum of 65." The **125** figure is better supported: the Early Creature Creator also starts with **125 DNA** when it is opened from the main menu.
- **DNA sources:** meat chunks (Jaw only); green plant matter (Filter Mouth or Proboscis); live peer-sized or smaller cells drained with a Proboscis (a peer cell can be fed on up to 3 times from full health); prey cells swallowed whole; and Maa eggs (any mouth). Eating prey-sized **Juniors never gives DNA**.
- Speedrunner notes say **free-floating** plants and meat give more progress than plants attached to larger structures.

### 2.1.6 Food and environmental objects
| Object | Mechanics |
|---|---|
| Small green plant matter | Filter Mouth or Proboscis. Present in every level. |
| Large plant flakes (level 2.5–4) | A cluster of edible bits around a centrepiece. They attract herbivores, which makes them good ambush spots. After the player grows, the whole flake counts as small food. |
| Seaweed (level 4+) | Food with obstacles. It shrinks by level 5. |
| Meat chunks | **Jaw only**. A Proboscis cannot eat meat chunks or dead cells. Killed cells usually burst into **3** chunks (the wiki gives a range of 1–4). Oversized meat must be cut with a Spike or Jaw. Cells killed by poison, electricity or a proboscis do not burst. They "flip over" and must be spiked or bitten open. |
| Eggs (level 5) | Laid by Maa, which always drops eggs when killed. Any mouth can eat small eggs, including herbivores. Large eggs cannot be eaten, but a Proboscis can eat the shells. |
| Crystals, debris, shells | Unbreakable barriers. They can be pushed around and used to block predators. |
| Bubbles | Can be popped. Large ones need a spike. |
| Currents | Ripples in the water. Swimming with them is faster and against them is slower. |
| Poison clouds | Deal damage over time. A cell carrying Poison is immune. |
| Electric sparks | Stun and damage. A cell carrying Electric is immune. |
| **Meteor shards** | Swimming over one breaks it open and unlocks a part. No weapon is needed. Shards stop spawning once every part is collected. |

### 2.1.7 Health, damage and cell size classes
- All cells normally have **6 health points**. The player's health is shown only as a percentage gauge.
- When the player dies, they respawn as a **newborn cell of the same species** somewhere else in the pool. Nothing is lost.
- **Four size classes** are measured relative to the player:
  - **Prey:** smaller and fragile. A Jaw or Proboscis swallows them whole.
  - **Peer:** the same size. They burst into about 3 meat chunks. StrategyWiki/FAQ: about 2 hits on Normal, 3 on Hard.
  - **Predator:** larger, and immune to harmful *mouths* but vulnerable to spikes, poison and electricity. StrategyWiki says they "can kill you in two strikes." The only predator-size herbivores are Squiggly and Maa.
  - **Epic:** the largest. They kill the player in one bite and do not appear on Easy.

| Attacker size | Damage to the player's cell |
|---|---|
| Epic | 100% (instant kill) |
| Predator | 50% |
| Peer | 20% |

| Player part | vs Predator | vs Peer | vs Prey |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spike | 8 | 3 | 0 |
| Jaw | 5 | 3 | 6 |
| Poison | 0 | 3 | 6 |
| Electric | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| Proboscis | 1 | 3 | 6 |

Poison and Electric do nothing to cells that carry the same part. When two spikes collide, neither does damage. A spike that hits a jaw damages the jaw's owner, and the jaw does nothing.

### 2.1.8 Epic cells and their weaknesses
The six epic cells, from smallest to largest, are Stabber, Ducky, Jawhead, Puffish, Megamouth and Snorf. They first appear in the background and then shrink through predator and peer size as the player grows.

| Epic | Weakness (Spore Wiki) | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Stabber | Hit its tail with a Spike | Easy |
| Ducky | Poke out both eyes with a Spike. It flees but does not die. | Easy |
| Jawhead | Push a shell into its mouth. This is referenced in the game code and may be impossible in practice. | ? |
| Puffish | Shock it with Electric. It takes as many zaps as a predator cell. | Hard |
| Megamouth | Get it to eat a Puffish that was once epic-sized | Extremely hard |
| Snorf | Impale it with a Proboscis. It flees when damaged, which stops players farming it. | Very hard |

### 2.1.9 Cell parts (complete base-game list)
The player starts with **6** parts unlocked and can collect **6** more, for **12** total (StrategyWiki). The starting set is the Jaw, Filter Mouth, Flagellum and the three eye types. The Cell Stage is the **only stage where parts stack**: adding more copies strengthens the ability, except for eyes and the Cilia's turning bonus. Every cell part also carries over into the Early Creature Creator as a creature-stage ability.

| Part | Type | Cell-stage effect | DNA | How obtained | Creature-stage ability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Filter Mouth** | Mouth | Eats plants and eggs. Cannot harm cells. | 15 | Start | Herbivore diet, Bite 1, Sing 1, Mating Call |
| **Jaw** | Mouth | Eats meat chunks and prey. Bites (damage). | 15 | Start | Carnivore diet, Bite 1, Sing 1, Mating Call |
| **Proboscis** | Mouth | Impales and drains live cells, eats plants and eggshells. **Cannot** eat meat chunks or dead cells. | 25 | Kill Nosey or Bloato | Omnivore diet, Bite 1, Sing 1, Mating Call |
| **Flagellum** | Movement | Speed +1, smooth movement | 15 | Start | Sneak 1 |
| **Cilia** | Movement | Speed +1 and **fast turns** (does not stack) | 15 | Level 3+: Pinky, Punky, Stabbella, Bloato | Jump 1 |
| **Jet** | Movement | Speed +2, moves in bursts | 25 | Level 2+: Booster, Nosey, Jetster, Paa, Snorf | Sprint 1 |
| **Spike** | Weapon | Ram damage and defence. Breaks dead cells and bubbles. | 10 | Level 1.5: Pokey (scripted kill on Easy/Medium), Spiked Shyster, Stabber, Stabbella, Squiggly, Jawhead, Needle Buzzy, Puffish, Megamouth | Charge 1 |
| **Poison** | Weapon | Leaves a toxic trail; more sacs make a longer trail. Spinning makes a cloud. Gives poison immunity. | 15 | Level 2+: Squirty, Puffish, meteor shards | Spit 1 |
| **Electric** | Weapon | Periodically shocks every nearby cell, recharging every 5–10 s depending on how many are carried. Gives electric immunity. | 25 | Level 4+: Buzzy, Needle Buzzy | Charm 1 |
| **Beady / Stalk / Button Eye** | Sight | Wider and full-colour vision | 5 each | Start | Sight |

Notes:
- **Speed cap.** The wiki's herbivore strategy says flagella can be added "until you reach a movement speed of 5," the maximum. A Jet gives 2 speed for 25 DNA, which is cheaper per point than flagella (2 for 30 DNA), but flagella move more smoothly. NPC jets seem to give longer bursts than the player's.
- **Eyes.** Without eyes the edges of the screen go black and vision shrinks, although meteors are easier to spot against the dark. All eyes work the same way.
- **Killing for parts.** A golden shield next to a cell's name means it carries a part the player doesn't have. Killing a cell like that, or seeing it killed, drops a part card. A part card from a killed predator appears larger. The manual says parts come from conquering "a peer or prey cell that has a part icon."
- **Hidden parts.** Some NPC cells carry cut "upgrade" variants (for example the Long Eye and alternate mouths, spikes and jets). They can only be used through the hidden **Advanced Cell Editor** (`-state:CellEditor` launch option) or mods. These variants may not give the normal creature-stage abilities.

### 2.1.10 The Cell Editor (Cell Creator)
- **Access.** Press **Call Mate**, follow the answering call to a mate with floating hearts, and swim over it. After a "beautiful courtship ritual, an egg is produced" (manual) and the Cell Creator opens. When the player returns, their cell hatches from the egg in a new place.
- The manual says Call Mate unlocks after the **first part is collected**. The Spore Wiki says it unlocks after **14 or more DNA**.
- Mating is a useful escape on Hard: predators stop chasing once the cutscene plays, and the player returns somewhere else. Calling a mate at a growth point spawns the mate nearby.
- **Editor rules.** The view is top-down. The body is a fixed **6 vertebrae** that can't be stretched; each vertebra can be made fatter or thinner. Parts can only be placed on the sides of the body.
- A **Complexity Meter** caps the number of parts. Complexity per mouth: Jaw 4, Filter Mouth 5, Proboscis 5.
- Paint mode offers **24 styles** and **735 colours**, and more styles appear as the stage progresses.
- **Refunds.** Removing a part refunds its full cost.
- Mouth stacking: putting a Jaw and a Filter Mouth, or a Proboscis and a Jaw, on the same cell lets it eat everything. A Proboscis with a Jaw lets the cell drain prey alive and then eat the meat.
- Holding A (Patch 1.05+) allows asymmetric, unmirrored placement.

### 2.1.12 NPC cell AI
- Cells have no relationship meter. Their behaviour is **hard-coded per species**.
- Prey-sized cells flee. Non-herbivore peers attack prey. Most non-herbivore predators attack peers but ignore prey. Epics attack peers and predators.
- Herbivores ignore each other but flee from non-herbivores and from anything that hurts them. A herbivore bumped into will "mumble and glare."
- Once the player has **Poison**, most peers flee from them. Exceptions include Junior swarms (aggressive even at prey size), Squiggly (counterattacks with a harmless filter mouth), Snorf (flees when hurt), and Buzzy and Stabbella (neutral until hurt).
- An attacking predator shows orange "growl" lightning bolts and triggers aggression music.

### 2.1.13 Diet trait card (Cell consequence)
- Every food item moves a meter on the **History/timeline** (T). Plants push it toward the **green herbivore** end. Meat, prey and proboscis-draining push it toward the **red carnivore** end. A balance ends in the **blue omnivore** middle.
- StrategyWiki says the arrow starts in the middle and that "actions later on in the cell stage have less of an effect on your final diet than ones earlier." The Spore Wiki says the result depends on **how many DNA points came from plants versus meat**, not on what was killed: a cell that kills many others but doesn't eat them can still be a herbivore.
- A common way to get the Omnivore card is to eat as a herbivore until the bar is half full and then switch to eating meat.
- The card sets the **mouths available in the Creature Creator** for the rest of the Creature Stage. Mouths of other diets are locked.
- The card also grants one consequence ability for each later stage:

| Cell card | Creature | Tribal | Civilization | Space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Herbivore** (green) | **Siren Song**: enthrals nearby creatures, calms attackers and makes socializing much easier, even with hostile species. No effect on Epics. | Refreshing Storm (fruit regrows and falls) | Healing Aura (repairs vehicles and buildings) | Social Suave (20% discount on social tools) |
| **Omnivore** (blue) | **Summon Flock**: calls a flock of small winged minions (about 10–15 HP each) to help fight or socialize | Flying Fish (the sea monster drives fish ashore) | Static Bomb (freezes vehicles, turrets and buildings) | Gentle Generalist (20% discount on standard tools) |
| **Carnivore** (red) | **Raging Roar**: makes nearby creatures run in fear. Does not affect Epics, Rogues, the player's own species or the pack. | Traps | Invulnerability (vehicles) | Power Monger (in practice +50% energy capacity) |

None of these abilities changes the relationship meter directly. Siren Song only makes impressing easier.

### 2.1.15 Transition to land
1. When the Progress Bar fills, a cutscene shows the cell **growing a brain**. The manual and speedrun notes call the cutscene "Growing a Brain."
2. The History icon glows. Clicking it, or the "Advance" button, ends the stage, shows the timeline review and awards the **diet trait card**.
3. The **Early Creature Creator** opens. It contains only the cell parts the player unlocked, plus three basic legs: **Brawnysaurus, Phatella and Thundercalf** (50 DNA each, Speed 1, basic "pad" feet). Legs are optional, and a creature can stay a legless "slug."
4. Cell-only parts can never be bought again later. The Flagellum gives Sneak for 15 DNA, which is cheaper than any later Sneak part.
5. **Omnivore loophole.** The Early Creature Creator is the only chance to fit a mouth from a diet other than the card. Keeping a Jaw plus a Filter Mouth, or a Proboscis, makes the creature eat both foods for the whole game. The card still decides which new mouths unlock later.
6. A cutscene shows a group of the species crawling out of the sea onto the beach and scattering. The Creature Stage begins.

## 2.2 CREATURE STAGE

### 2.2.1 Overview
- The stage is a third-person 3D open world on **one continent** of the homeworld. Swimming into deep water (dark on the minimap) triggers a cutscene in which the unkillable **Sea Monster** eats the player. It never harms pack members.
- The goal is to earn DNA by **befriending or hunting** other species. DNA grows the brain until the species becomes sapient and can enter the Tribal Stage.
- The *Speed Demon* achievement is for finishing in under an hour. A Speed Demos Archive run finished the stage in about **11 minutes on Easy**.
- The manual says "In the Creature stage, you do not have to protect your nest." The home nest is where the player **respawns** and **heals**.

| Jump; flap wings (Glide) | Spacebar |

The HUD shows objectives, a minimap, the DNA Progress Bar, History, **social and combat stance buttons**, the **consequence ability** button, **health and hunger meters**, and portraits of pack members.

### 2.2.3 Brain (intelligence) levels, health and pack size
The manual says: "When you gain enough DNA to fill the Progress Bar across a vertical milestone line, you automatically jump to the next intelligence level," and "you may then add another creature to your pack—up to three creatures." A Steam guide counts **three milestone markers** on the bar. The GameFAQs-derived FAQ says the stage is completed by reaching **level 5** and gives these HP values:

| Brain level | Player max HP (FAQ) | Pack slots |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (landfall) | 10 | 0 |
| 2 | 15 | 1 |
| 3 | 25 | 2 |
| 4 | 40 | 3 (maximum) |
| 5 (bar full, sapient) | 100 | 3 |

- The Spore Wiki gives the same endpoints. A new creature has **10 HP**, and a full-brain creature has **100–110 HP** depending on Health parts.
- A brain level-up refills health and hunger (FAQ) and makes the creature physically larger (Spore Wiki).
- The Spore Wiki's *Posse* page agrees: "a posse of 1 can be recruited once the first brain level is reached… maximum of three."
- Reaching certain milestones triggers **migrations** (see 2.2.13).
- After the bar is full the player can keep playing. Ally and extinction rewards drop sharply, except for Rogues, Epics and eggs, but the *Max Power* achievement can still be worked toward.

| Hostile wandering hunting pack (first migration) | About 35 when exterminated |
| Selling a part | Full refund |

Speedrunners note that allying neutral species gives more DNA than making them extinct. Species that start out angry pay more to kill but are harder to wipe out.

### 2.2.5 Health, hunger, food and diet
- **Hunger bar** (orange, under the health bar). Movement burns energy. When the bar empties, the creature **starves** and loses HP until it dies.
- A newly hatched creature always has full hunger. One wiki tip says a player could in theory finish the stage without eating by mating repeatedly.
- **Food types:**
  - **Fruit** grows on bushes and small trees. It rots if left too long. High fruit needs height and **graspers**, or a thrown object to knock it down.
  - **Meat** comes from corpses, which also rot. The first nest has a corpse beside it. A dead Epic carries a huge amount of meat.
  - **Eggs** can be eaten by any diet, but not the player's own species' eggs.
- **Diet comes from the mouths currently fitted**, not from the card. Herbivore mouths eat fruit, carnivore mouths eat meat, and omnivore mouths (or one herbivore plus one carnivore mouth) eat both.
- Eating the wrong food makes the creature **vomit** and gives no nutrition.
- The FAQ says herbivores heal instantly from fruit, carnivores heal over time from corpses, and omnivores get reduced effects from both.
- **Healing.** Health regenerates by eating, by resting in the home nest, or by sitting in any **Friendly or Allied** nest, or the nest of an **extinct** species.
- If the player dies, they are reborn at the home nest. If they die during a migration, they respawn at the old nest. The pack comes back with them.

### 2.2.6 The Creature Creator (in-stage)
- **Access.** Use the Mating Call, go to the swooning mate at the nest (pink hearts), and click it. An egg is laid and the editor opens. Changes apply to the **whole species**. The player then hatches as a **baby** and "learns" any new abilities.
- If the nest is empty, the Mating Call spawns a mate. The mate "always responds from your current nest" (manual), so the call also works as a compass home. Using it near hostile nests draws attention.
- The base game has **228 functional parts** in **7 palettes**: mouths, eyes/senses, limbs, graspers (hands), feet, weapons and details. Each palette has up to 4 columns, or tiers.
- **Tiered parts** cost **25 / 75 / 150 / 250 DNA** from Tier 1 to Tier 4. Eyes and sensory parts cost **10**. Limbs cost **50–65** (Layered Limb is 80). Bot parts cost 25–75.
- Parts lower in a column or further right are stronger.
- **Stats do not add across parts.** Each ability takes its value from the **highest-level part** granting it, and placement doesn't matter. The FAQ says "Abilities don't stack." This differs from the Cell Stage.
- Editor tricks: removing limb joints (Ctrl-click) saves DNA. Alt-drag duplicates a part. Tab shows extra rotation rings. Holding I shows a part's stats (Patch 1.02+). Holding A places parts asymmetrically (Patch 1.05+). B opens the Sporepedia.
- Keeping a cell-stage eye saves 5 DNA, because all eyes work identically.

| 5 | 3 | 3 / 7 s | 9 | 2.5 + 3.1 |

- **Bite** has the highest sustained damage at the same level and is the attack the AI uses most.
- **Charge** only works from range and stuns for 3 s. When two creatures charge each other, the higher level wins. Spitting at a charging creature stuns it.
- **Strike** hits several targets. Secondary targets take reduced damage and do not count toward extinction.
- **Spit** is the only ranged attack. It homes, knocks gliding creatures out of the air, and provokes the target to chase the player, which pulls it away from its nest.
- **Sprint** gives about **+43%** speed at every level. Duration is 10/15/20/25/30 s for levels 1–5, the button cooldown is 25 s, and the stamina recharge is 170/130/100/75/60 s.
- **Sneak** makes the creature translucent. Higher levels let it get closer before being spotted, and the first attack from stealth gets a damage bonus. Attacking or socializing ends it. Epics cannot detect a sneaking creature, so Sneak is a way to loot their bone piles.
- **Jump** gets higher with level, and holding the key extends it. **Glide** gives more "flaps" per level. *Flight of the Bumblebee* is awarded for flying 200 m without touching the ground.

The base game has **12 carnivore, 16 omnivore and 12 herbivore mouths**. *Creepy & Cute* adds 4 per diet and more parts in every category. The **Bot Parts** (Patch 1.06, Dr Pepper promotion) include two mouths with **no Sing**. Socializing as or with a no-Sing creature can **crash** the game.

### 2.2.7 Unlocking parts: skeletons (bone piles) and alphas
- Clicking a skeleton makes the creature **dig** it up with hands, or with mouth or feet if it has none. A golden part shield then appears.
- Skeletons come in **4 sizes**, and larger piles give higher-tier parts:
  1. Partial ribcage, found anywhere.
  2. Full ribcage, found anywhere.
  3. Larger, more exposed skeletons. Often found near the post-migration nest.
  4. Exposed ribcage with a Slagjawed-type skull. Found near **Epics**, high-level nests and migration nests.
- **Upgrade priority.** The game favours unlocking the **next tier of whatever style is currently equipped**. Fitting a low-tier mouth makes its line upgrade first. StrategyWiki notes that some cell parts unlock specific lines, so a herbivore mouth's shield unlocks the herbivore column.
- If every part in a tier has been found, the pile gives a lower-tier part. When all parts are found, skeletons disappear.
- **Alphas** are marked by a gold star in a pentagon. Killing or befriending one drops a part. Crashed spaceships usually have strong parts nearby.

### 2.2.8 Socialization mini-game
1. Switch to **social stance** and double-click a creature, or press a number key. The target's relationship must be at least **Annoyed** (orange). **Hostile** (red) species cannot be socialized unless **Siren Song** is active. **Epics can never be socialized.**
2. A semicircular meter appears with the target's relation face above it. The target performs Sing, Dance, Charm or Pose at **its own level** and fills **its** half (right side).
3. The player must **mirror** the same action at an "equal or superior" level (manual) to fill the left side. Using a different action fills less and disappoints the target. If the target fills its half first, the attempt fails. A failed attempt does not lower the relationship and can be retried.
4. Higher social levels fill faster, and pack members with that ability add to the player's power. Summon Flock minions can help too.
5. Higher-level species call **nest-mates** to watch, which makes the meter harder to fill. Speedrunners pull targets away from their nests to avoid this.
6. Speedrunners observed that a **jump** can instantly fill the player's side.
7. A successful impression puts a **starburst** around the target's face, and it cannot be socialized again. Impressing **2 members** of a species makes it **Friendly** (blue), and **3** makes it an **Ally** (green). A **Rogue** becomes an Ally after **one** success.
8. Allies, or members of the player's own species, join the pack by socializing with them again if a slot is free. Lovestruck mates can also be recruited by pressing a social key instead of mating.
9. **Alphas** take one more socialization round than normal members.

### 2.2.9 Relationships, AI and territory
| Face | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green (Ally) | 3 befriended. Can join the pack. The player can heal at their nest and can no longer attack them or their eggs. |
| Blue (Friendly) | 2 befriended. The player can heal at their nest. |
| Yellow (Curious) | Neutral. Approaches and crowds the player. Typical of herbivores and some omnivores. |
| Orange (Annoyed) | Watches, growls and may attack intruders in its territory. Typical of carnivores and some omnivores. |
| Fear | The player killed a member of a curious or friendly species, or the Alpha of an annoyed one. The species flees on sight but can still be cornered and befriended. |
| Red (Hostile) | Attacks within seconds and cannot be befriended without Siren Song. Caused by killing a **baby**, **eating an egg**, or by nature (carnivores and strong omnivores on Medium and Hard). Wandering hunting packs are always hostile. |
| Pink (Lovestruck) | A mate at the player's own nest |

- **Starting attitudes** depend on diet, stats and difficulty. On Easy, carnivores start Annoyed. On Medium and Hard, level-2-plus carnivores and strong omnivores are often Hostile.
- **NPC levels 1–4.** Level 1 has at most 1 social and 2 combat points and is never hostile. Level 2 has at least 2 social points and lays eggs and raises babies. Level 3 has at least 7 points in both categories, or 5 in one, and travels in groups to patrol and find water. Level 4 has 18 stat points, or one category above 11.
- NPC health runs from about 6 to 50 by level. Speedrunners treat 15 and 19 HP as level 2. Babies have half HP, and Alphas about 25% more.
- **Cocoons.** When the player's brain grows, especially early on, weaker nests may spin cocoons and hatch a stronger replacement species a few minutes later. These can be interacted with about 30 s after hatching.
- Attacking any member worsens the relationship with the whole species. Spitting at a creature on the edge of a nest pulls it away alone.
- Luring a strong enemy to the player's **home nest** makes nest-mates join the fight.
- Thrown objects (graspers or mouth): **sticks** only annoy ("@#$%!"). **Shells** and **rocks** stun. Objects given as gifts in social stance do **not** improve relationships.

### 2.2.10 Hunting and extinction
- Killing enough members of a species makes it **extinct**. The rest float up in sparkles and vanish for the rest of the stage. The nest gets a skull and crossbones on the minimap and becomes a healing spot, and all of that species' eggs break.
- The wiki says it takes **3–7 kills** depending on the species' level. A speedrunner wiped out an early nest with **4 kills**.
- Extinction and alliance both give large one-off DNA bonuses.
- Wiping out a species can make a matching Epic or Rogue **afraid** of the player.

| Type | HP | Behaviour |

Common ways to kill an Epic: **kite it with Spit** from range or from high ground, which can take 5–10 minutes, so eat to avoid starving. Circle close to its ankles and attack while it turns slowly. Get it stuck in trees. Pull it to the home nest. Lead two Epics into each other. If the player dies away from the fight, the Epic **fully heals**.

"Adventurer creatures" are not a Creature Stage creature type in any source consulted. *Adventurer* is a *Galactic Adventures* achievement.

### 2.2.12 Pack (posse)
- A pack holds **up to 3** members: the player's own species, allied species, or allied Rogues. Members follow the player and help fight and socialize, adding their social skills to the player's.
- Pack members have **no hunger bar**. They return with the player on respawn, but if they die they must be replaced.
- To flee with the pack: switch to social stance, pause, and double-click pack members so they follow.
- Pack members of **other species** become **pets** in the Tribal Stage, up to 3. Summon Flock minions present at evolution can also become pets.
- Achievements: *Village Folks* (3 pack members from different species) and *General Custer* (30 posse deaths, which can be spread across games, and Summon Flock minions count).

### 2.2.13 Mating, generations and migration
- Every trip to the editor creates a **new generation**. The player hatches as a baby. A baby in the pack grows to adult at the next mating.
- **Migration.** At set brain milestones, the first soon after the first brain level, nest-mates leave for a new nest. A dirt path or dotted line on the map and the Mating Call show the way.
- Small nests along the route become **respawn points** when clicked.
- Reaching the new nest plays a cutscene, enlarges the nest and pays **25–50 DNA**. There are **two migrations** per stage.
- New nests often have an Alpha of the player's own species and high-tier skeletons nearby.
- Scripted events block pack recruitment and mating. Entering the editor before a migration is "ready" can force a second visit.
- Before advancing, it is wise to settle at a nest **near water** (but not on the shore). This helps fishing in the Tribal Stage and a coastal city later.

### 2.2.14 World events and environment
- **Meteor shower** (mid-stage): a flash and a big impact, then fragments fall. A hit does 1 damage and stuns. Creatures panic for about 30 s, which leaves eggs unguarded and creatures easier to kill.
- **Spaceship/UFO**: flies over and **abducts** creatures, which flee in panic. This can ruin an extinction or alliance count. Clicking the ship adds it to the Sporepedia.
- **Rain**: purely visual (lightning, sunbeams). Creatures may shelter under trees.
- **Day/night cycle**: purely visual, and nights are shorter than days (StrategyWiki).
- **Vents** (spice geysers): launch the creature into the air.

### 2.2.15 Population from the Sporepedia
- The Creature Stage world is filled with creature designs from the Sporepedia: the player's own creations, downloads, and Maxis creatures. Wikipedia calls the design "massively single-player" with "asynchronous sharing." Launch-era descriptions say content is matched to the environment and the player's progress.
- The **Socialite** achievement counts this: "Meet 200 creatures made by other players." Clicking a new species to see its card counts as meeting it. Maxis creations don't count, but the player's own other creations do.
- Players can **ban** creations so they never appear again.
- The game assigns Epic, Rogue and nest levels to these designs based on their stats.

### 2.2.16 Creature consequence cards (archetype path)
The History timeline drifts **down toward red** for aggressive actions and **up toward green** for social ones. Balanced play gives **blue**.

| Card | How earned | Tribal | Civilization | Space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Social** (green) | Mostly befriending and allying | Fireworks (raises relations with nearby tribes) | Diplo Dervish | Pleasing Performance (colonies happier, fewer revolts) |
| **Adaptable** (blue) | A mix of allies and extinctions | Beastmaster (chieftain enchants nearby animals to help) | Bribe Bomb (enemy vehicles attack each other) | Speed Demon (faster interstellar travel) |
| **Predator** (red) | Mostly killing and extinctions | Fire Bombs | Mighty Bomb | Prime Specimen (ship health ×1.5) |

- The Creature card grants **no** Creature-stage ability. The only Creature-stage consequence ability is the one from the **Cell** card (Siren Song, Summon Flock or Raging Roar). It sits above the Progress Bar and has a long recharge.
- A player who **starts a new game at the Creature Stage** skips the Cell Stage and gets **no** cell consequence ability. They also have no diet restriction from a card.
- The FAQ maps each cell card to a natural creature path: herbivore with Siren Song to Social, carnivore with Raging Roar to Predator, and omnivore with Summon Flock to Adaptable.

### 2.2.17 Advancing to the Tribal Stage
- When the bar is full, a final "Growing a Brain" cutscene plays, referencing *2001: A Space Odyssey*. The History icon glows. Clicking it shows the card review, then the **final edit**.
- After the final edit, **the body design is permanent**. Later changes are clothing only, made in outfitters.
- Combat and social abilities carry over. Tribe members sometimes fight with their natural attacks, and *Galactic Adventures* uses all of them.
- Other-species pack members become pets.

## 2.3 Frequently misunderstood mechanics
1. **The diet card and the actual diet are separate.** The card only decides which mouths **unlock** in the Creature Creator. What a creature can eat depends on the mouths fitted. A carnivore-card creature that kept a Filter Mouth from the Early Creature Creator can still eat fruit.
2. **No Jaw is needed for carnivore mouths.** Any cell that ends with a carnivore card unlocks them, including a Proboscis cell that ate mostly meat.
3. **The Proboscis can't eat meat chunks or corpses**, even after a spike breaks them open. It drains living cells, eats plants, and eats eggshells.
4. **Killing isn't eating.** A cell's trait comes from what it eats, so a cell that kills a lot can still finish as a herbivore.
5. **All eyes are identical.** Sight is on or off, and eye type is cosmetic in both stages.
6. **Creature stats don't stack.** Only the best part for each ability counts. In the Cell Stage, parts **do** stack.
7. **Pack slots come from brain growth, not DNA spent.** The maximum is 3.
8. **Siren Song doesn't raise relationships.** It enthrals creatures and makes impressing easier, including hostile species, but never affects Epics. Raging Roar doesn't affect Epics or Rogues either.
9. **Gifts and thrown sticks don't change relationships.** Killing babies or eating eggs makes a species permanently hostile, unless Raging Roar's fear effect is active when the egg is eaten.
10. **Epics can't be socialized.** They become friendly only if the player allies the matching normal species.
11. **Dying near an Epic resets it** to full health if it's out of view when the player respawns.
12. **Cell-only parts are a bargain.** The Flagellum gives Sneak 1 for 15 DNA, and the Electric part gives Charm 1. They can only be fitted in the Early Creature Creator and are lost once removed. The wiki considers Electric poor value at 25 DNA.
13. **Excess DNA is lost** between stages. The Cell Stage cap is 125 total, according to the DNA page.
