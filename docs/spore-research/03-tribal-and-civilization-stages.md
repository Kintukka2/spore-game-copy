# 3. Tribal Stage and Civilization Stage: Gameplay Mechanics

Spore's third and fourth stages turn the game into a real-time strategy (RTS) game. In the **Tribal Stage** you manage a whole tribe of your species: you gather food, build tool huts, and win over or wipe out five rival tribes. In the **Civilization Stage** you run a nation of cities and vehicles, earn Sporebucks from spice and factories, and try to bring every city on your homeworld under your control by military, religious or economic means. Both stages award a **consequence trait card**. Each card grants abilities in later stages and helps decide your Space Stage **archetype**.

> Note on terminology: in the Civilization Stage the **currency is Sporebucks (§)**. Spice is the resource that **spice geysers** produce. Claiming a geyser with a **spice derrick** turns its output into Sporebucks per minute. Spice becomes a tradable commodity only in the Space Stage.

## 3.1 Tribal Stage

### 3.1.1 Overview and entry

- The Tribal Stage is the third of Spore's five stages. It follows the Creature Stage and precedes the Civilization Stage. The wiki gives its genre as real-time strategy and its unit as the tribe.
- When your creature evolves sapience, it can advance to Tribal. A cutscene shows the future **Chieftain** discovering fire with a stick, set to the music from *2001: A Space Odyssey* and referencing that film's tool-discovery scene. The stick then becomes the Chieftain's staff.
- **The creature's body becomes permanent at this point.** The last creator session before Tribal is the final chance to add or remove body parts. From here on you can only change clothing (in the outfitters) and colours (paint mode).
- **Creature-stage carry-over:** every animal of another species in your **pack** when you evolve becomes a **domesticated pet** in the Tribal Stage. You can get more than three pets this way by using *Summon Flock* just before evolving.
- Wikipedia notes that diet choices from earlier stages decide which foods the tribe can gather. Diet also sets the starting tools (see 3.1.6) and the look of the main hut: herbivores get clay with a leafy roof, omnivores get wood, and carnivores get animal skin on a horned wooden frame.

### 3.1.2 The village

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| **Chief's hut (main hut / Tribal Hut)** | Command centre. From here you buy babies, open the **Tribal Planner** and the **Tribal Outfitter**. AI tribes are defeated by destroying this hut. It becomes the **City Hall** in the Civilization Stage. |
| **Tool huts (tool shacks)** | Each one supplies one tool type. You place them in the Tribal Planner. There are **6 placement slots**, so at most 6 of the 9 tool types can be built at once. You can remove a hut to get food back or to make room for another. |
| **Food mat / food pile** | Stockpile of the tribe's food (the stage currency). Rivals and wild animals may raid it. |
| **Fire pit** | Village centre. Tribe members right-clicked onto it dance around it. |
| **Tribal totem** | Progress meter. **A head is added each time you ally with or defeat a tribe.** With five heads you can advance to the Civilization Stage (StrategyWiki). Wikipedia says each piece can raise the population cap or unlock tools and clothes. |
| **Pet pen and egg bin** | Holds up to 3 domesticated animals, whose eggs pile up in the bin. |

**Main hut upgrades.** The hut upgrades as you ally with or conquer tribes. Each upgrade makes the hut tougher and lets the tribe grow larger.
- SporeWiki: **400 HP** at the start, **750 HP** after the first upgrade, **1,000 HP** after the second. StrategyWiki gives 500 / 750 / 1,000.
- Population cap (GameRevolution FAQ): **6 members** at the start, **9 after the first tribe** is dealt with, **12 after the third**. The guide says **12** is the most units you can control.

### 3.1.3 Tribe members and the Chieftain

- **Babies cost 10 food each** (SporeWiki, StrategyWiki, GameRevolution, and a speedrun budget of 6 babies for 60 food). An egg rolls out of the hut and hatches. StrategyWiki says the baby becomes an adult **after 30 seconds**. Babies have half health, can't be controlled until they are adults, and are favourite targets for raiders and wild animals.
- **Tools.** Members pick up a tool by being sent to the matching tool hut. They never do this on their own. Each member holds **one tool** at a time. Members can do any task with or without a tool, except playing an instrument or healing, but the right tool makes its task much more efficient.
- Members without weapons fight with the natural abilities of their creature parts. Members without instruments join performances as **backup dancers**, adding a small bonus to the social meter.
- **Stance.** The tribe switches between a **social stance** and an **attack stance**. Social stance is used for gifts, domestication and music. Attack stance is used for stealing and fighting.
- **Behaviour reflects your play.** Aggressive tribes roughhouse and push each other while idle. Peaceful tribes are gentler.
- **Starvation.** Members eat automatically while gathering if they are hungry. Starving members slowly lose health. You need **at least one member alive** (a regular member or the Chieftain) to stay in the game.

**The Chieftain**
- Has **50% more health** (1.5×) than a normal member and **respawns at the hut about one minute** after dying.
- Cannot equip tools. The staff works as a **gathering cane, fishing spear and club**. It deals **12 damage to creatures and 7 to buildings** and knocks targets down.
- Is required to **conduct the band** when socializing and to **use consequence abilities**. It is not required for attacks.
- Gift-giving is led by the Chieftain.
- The player's Chieftain is always left-handed.

### 3.1.4 Food: the Tribal Stage currency

Food replaces DNA as the currency. You spend it on babies, tool huts, gifts and domestication. The Food article gives a maximum stockpile of about 8,000.

| Food | How you get it | Who can eat it |
|---|---|---|
| **Fruit** | Picked from shrubs or small trees. A **Gathering Cane** bashes the tree so all its fruit drops. | Herbivores, omnivores |
| **Meat** | Butchered from hunted creatures. An **Epic creature** gives 100–300 food. | Carnivores, omnivores |
| **Fish / Seaweed** | Fishing at rippling-water spots on the coast, marked with a fish icon on the minimap. Herbivores bring back seaweed. | All diets (by type) |
| **Eggs** | Laid by pets into the egg bin, or stolen from wild nests (which turns that species hostile). | All diets |

- **Carrying capacity.** A base of **5 units per trip**, plus 1 for each Gathering level from clothing, up to **10**.
- **Fishing Spear.** Raises the catch by 200%: **3 food per catch instead of 1**.
- **Stealing.** In attack stance, click a rival's food pile, which shows a bandit-mask cursor. A successful raid brings back as much as the member can carry. Stealing hurts relations and provokes any witnesses. The *Sneak* creature ability improves the odds of success.
- **Theft against you.** Hostile tribes and scavenging animals steal from an unguarded pile. A warning message appears first. GameRevolution says animals steal at about **1 food per 2 seconds**.
- **Looting.** After a rival's main hut is razed, you can harvest its remaining food.
- **Food triggers rival tribes.** SporeWiki says the next three rival tribes appear once your stockpile reaches **70 food**, even if the first rival tribe hasn't been dealt with yet.

### 3.1.5 Costs (food)

|---|---|---|
| New tribe member (baby) | **10** | Consistent across sources |
| Tool hut | **25** (GameRevolution: "All buildings cost 25 food"; a speedrun budget gives 3 buildings for 75) | SporeWiki's Music and Fishing Spear pages say **20**. 25 is better supported. |
| Gift to another tribe | **10** | SporeWiki and GameRevolution. Gamepressure says 15. Each gift raises relations by about +10. |

### 3.1.6 Tool huts and tools

There are nine tools in three families. All three instruments work the same way; they differ only in which instrument a tribe asks to hear. Healing Rod wielders get the prefix "Shaman".

| Family | Tool | Mechanics (SporeWiki values) |
|---|---|---|
| **Instruments (social)** | Wooden Horn (hotkey 1), Maraca (hotkey 2), Didgeridoo (hotkey 3) | Played during socialization concerts. Rivals only request instruments you have unlocked. |
| **Weapons** | **Stone Axe** | 9 damage to creatures, 5 to huts and tool shacks. Best against creatures. |
| | **Throwing Spear** | Ranged: 10 damage per throw, 5 in melee. Thrown spears do no damage to buildings or babies. Best weapon against Epics. |
| | **Flaming Torch** | 6 damage to creatures, 9 to huts and shacks. The torch button sets a target on fire for +3 damage and stuns it briefly. Best for razing huts. |
| **Food & healing** | **Fishing Spear** | Triples the fish or seaweed catch. Can't be used in combat. |
| | **Gathering Cane (Gathering Rod)** | Knocks all fruit off a tree. Not available to carnivores. |
| | **Healing Rod** | Heals nearby injured members and pets at about 1 HP per second. Does 3 damage per hit if forced into combat. |

**Tool unlock order** depends on diet and on *how many* tribes you have dealt with. It doesn't matter whether you allied or destroyed them. Up to 9 tools can be unlocked (8 for carnivores, who never get Gathering Canes), but only 6 huts fit in the village. SporeWiki and StrategyWiki give the same table:

| Tribes dealt with | Herbivore | Omnivore | Carnivore |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Flaming Torches, Maracas | Throwing Spears, Wooden Horns | Stone Axes, Didgeridoos |
| 1 | Gathering Canes, Didgeridoos | Fishing Spears, Maracas | Fishing Spears, Horns |
| 2 | Throwing Spears, Horns | Gathering Canes, Healing Rods, Didgeridoos | Throwing Spears |
| 3 | Fishing Spears, Healing Rods | Flaming Torches | Flaming Torches, Maracas |
| 4 | Stone Axes | Stone Axes | Healing Rods |

Every diet's intro cutscene shows the creature holding a torch, but only herbivores start with Flaming Torches.

### 3.1.7 Tribal Outfitter (clothing) and stat bonuses

- **Access.** Through the Tribal Planner (helmet icon). The outfitter replaces the Creature Creator. You can't change the body any more, only add accessories and repaint.
- **Budget.** The SporeWiki Outfitters page says you start with a budget of **100** and gain **25** for each tribe you conquer. It also gives a full outfitter budget of 200, which can be raised by 150 with the `addDNA` cheat.
- **Content.** 46 accessories: 10 hats, 6 masks, 8 chest pieces, 6 shoulders, 6 symbols and 10 details. More parts unlock as you deal with other tribes.
- **Four stats: Gathering, Social, Combat and Health.** Each Gathering level adds +1 to carrying capacity (base 5). Each Health level adds **+2 HP**. Social and Combat levels give small multipliers to socializing and to damage (GameRevolution). Wearing the best social clothing speeds up the social meter.
- **Symbols and details** give no stat bonuses. Symbols cost 2 points each and details cost 1.

The Civilization Outfitter has a budget of 1,000 and the Space Outfitter also gives 1,000. Their clothing is **purely cosmetic**.

### 3.1.8 Domestication and pets

- **Domestication costs 15 food.** In social stance, right-click a land creature. A member feeds it a bone-shaped treat and leads it to the pen.
- After one member of a species accepts a treat, the rest of that species is friendly and can be domesticated again for free. Attacking that species reverses this. **Killing any baby or egg of a species makes it permanently hostile.**
- **Up to 3 pets.** Further domestication attempts only make the species friendly.
- **Eggs.** SporeWiki says three pets lay one platter of eggs about **every 30 seconds**, and a full egg bin holds up to **150 food**. Eggs accumulate without limit while you do other things.
- **Defence.** Pets fight raiders. Rogue creatures carried over from the Creature Stage make strong pets with 250 HP. Pets can't heal themselves; tribesmen with Healing Rods heal them.
- **UFO visits.** At some point a spaceship visits and may abduct pets, and rarely tribe members or even the Chieftain. It hovers near the Chieftain, so moving the Chieftain away protects the village.
- **Achievement.** *Domestic Bliss*: domesticate and farm three different species.

### 3.1.9 Rival tribes

- **Five rival tribes** form over the course of the stage, each a different species. The usual sequence is **one tribe of 6, then three tribes of 9, then one tribe of 12**.
- AI tribes sleep at night (shown by "Z"s from their hut), which leaves them vulnerable to night raids. They can repopulate from food even after losing every member, and their Chieftains respawn.

| Difficulty | Rival behaviour (SporeWiki) |
|---|---|
| **Easy** | One sworn enemy that rarely attacks and sends only 3 raiders. Some tribes dislike you but seldom steal. Only one enemy attacks at a time. No carnivorous wild nests that dislike the tribe. |
| **Normal** | Two of the 9-member tribes dislike you and the 12-member tribe hates you. Raids of 4–6 members, or up to 7–8 from the final tribe. One attacker at a time. Occasional carnivorous nests. |
| **Hard** | The first tribe and one 9-member tribe dislike you and steal often. The rest hate you and send 5–8 raiders, or up to 10 from the final tribe, sometimes with their Chieftain. Several tribes may raid at once. Rivals respawn 2 members for free on short intervals. Carnivorous nests are common. |

**Relationship faces (Tribal):**

| Face | State | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Ally | Sends you regular food gifts. Never drops back. |
| Blue | Friendly | May drift back to neutral if ignored. Reached through music, it stays unless attacked or robbed. |
| Yellow | Neutral | Ignores you. Can be socialized with music. |
| Orange | Dislike | Steals food sometimes. Must be gifted before you can socialize. |
| Red | Enemy | Raids your village and tries to destroy your hut. |

### 3.1.10 Social conquest: gifts and music

1. **Gift.** In social stance, right-click the rival's food pile. This costs 10 food. The Chieftain carries a basket while members throw flowers, and the tribe's relationship rises by about +10. A gift to the food pile wears off over time (as little as about 2 minutes on Hard). Gifting a tribe member directly keeps them neutral until they are attacked.
2. **Music.** Bring the Chieftain plus instrument players; GameRevolution suggests 3 to 6 members. Tribe members request an instrument in a speech bubble, and you answer by pressing hotkey **1 (Horn), 2 (Maraca) or 3 (Didgeridoo)** or by selecting players. SporeWiki strategy notes describe **5 requests** per performance. Unequipped members dance for a small bonus. GameRevolution says one mistake is allowed when going from yellow to blue, but **a flawless performance is needed to reach green (allied)**. SporeWiki recommends about 3 players per instrument. It also warns against unlocking all three instruments before you can field 12 members, because the players get spread too thin.
3. **Ally.** At green the tribe becomes an ally. It adds a totem head, unlocks the next tools, huts and clothing, and starts sending you food.

- *Fireworks* (Social creature consequence) instantly makes an angry, disliking or neutral tribe friendly. The effect wears off over time and has no effect on tribes that are already friendly.
- *Beastmaster* (Adaptable) recruits nearby animals that help socialize or fight.

### 3.1.11 Aggressive conquest: raiding

- **To destroy a tribe, raze its main hut.** You can kill its members first (axes or spears) and then burn the hut with torches. Once the hut is gone, the remaining members flee in panic and die.
- Destroying a rival's weapon hut stops it from arming its members. Stealing its food stops it from making babies.
- SporeWiki strategy notes: let an enemy attack you first, kill its raiding party to reduce its population, then counter-attack; or attack at night. Leave someone at home to protect the food and babies.
- *Fire Bombs* (Predator creature consequence) deal 15 damage in an area and knock targets down. *Traps* (Carnivore cell consequence) kill everything lured to them with poison gas, Epics included.
- **Achievement.** *Vicious*: kill all members of all 5 other tribes and raze their villages.

### 3.1.12 Epic creatures in Tribal

Epics are giant, hostile versions of wild creatures. Usually one or two appear during the stage, and they sometimes wander into villages. Carnivores are the most likely species to be chosen as Epics and herbivores never are. A dead Epic gives well over 100 meat (100–300 food). Spearmen kiting from range or a Trap are the recommended ways to kill one. If an Epic is of your own species, it is automatically your ally.

### 3.1.13 Consequence abilities used in Tribal

The Chieftain gets **two** abilities, one from the Cell card and one from the Creature card. Each is cast by the Chieftain. Wikipedia describes this as receiving "two of six possible Consequence Abilities".

| Cell: Omnivore | Blue | **Flying Fish** | A sea serpent belly-flops and scatters fish on the shore (requires nearby water) |
| Cell: Carnivore | Red | **Traps** | A trap lures animals and kills them with gas (works on Epics) |

### 3.1.14 Ending the stage: the Tribal consequence card

The stage ends once all five rivals have been allied with or destroyed and the totem is complete. The card depends on where your **timeline trend bar** ends up (green ↔ red) across the stage.

| Card | Colour | How it is earned | Civ-stage effect | Civ ability | Space ability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Friendly** | Green | Socialize. SporeWiki's card page says all 5 tribes must be socialized. | Start as a **Religious** city | **Black Cloud**: a cloud rains diseased animals on a target city, shutting down its entertainment buildings and turrets and making citizens unhappy | **Gracious Greeting**: +10 initial relationship with alien empires |
| **Industrious** | Blue | A balance of allying and destroying. Watch the trend bar: GameRevolution says to destroy only one tribe if you were a Predator, or befriend only one if you were Social. | Start as an **Economic** city | **Ad Blitz**: a blimp instantly fills the target city's economic-takeover (buyout) bar | **Colony Craze**: 20% discount on colonization tools |

### 3.1.16 Tribal trivia and easter eggs

- Right-clicking the fire pit makes members dance a salsa to the mariachi song "El Gavilán".
- Idle members chat, party, toast marshmallows at the campfire, play ball, and mourn fallen comrades (and dead pets). Speech bubbles sometimes show the Spore galaxy icon or Will Wright's head.
- Just before the switch to Civilization, the tribe holds a debate in which one member suggests "pie" and the others look baffled.
- All the other sapient species disappear in the Civilization Stage. Every rival nation there is your own species.
- Commentators compare the tribal shouts and the "one member must survive" rule to *Populous: The Beginning*, and the controls to *Age of Mythology*.

## 3.2 Transition to Civilization

- On advancing, the tribe becomes a **city**. You design the **City Hall** in the Building Editor and your first **land vehicle** in the Vehicle Creator. The vehicle type matches the tribal card: religious, economic or military (GameRevolution 5.1).
- The **Founder** achievement is earned here.
- Food is replaced by **Sporebucks (§)**. The Clothing editor stays available but is purely cosmetic from now on.

## 3.3 Civilization Stage

### 3.3.1 Overview and goal

- The fourth stage, between Tribal and Space. Its genre is real-time strategy and its unit is the nation. Vehicles replace creatures as your units, and cities replace huts.
- **Goal:** bring every city on the planet under your control, whether by capture, conversion, purchase, merger or surrender. Then build a spaceship and launch it to begin the Space Stage.
- **Number of cities.** GameRevolution says to "gain control of all 10 cities on the planet". SporeWiki says the last enemy usually offers surrender once you hold **9** cities. A speedrun converted 7 cities and then paid for the superweapon at a cost matching 3 remaining cities. Coastal "sea cities" with docks appear; players can't build them.
- You start with **one city**. Rival nations emerge from tribes soon after; on Hard they appear very quickly. Each rival has one of the three specialties, which shapes how it treats you. SporeWiki says there are almost always four cities on your own continent.
- **Leftover tribes.** Small tribes remain at the start; there are more tribes than can become civilizations, nine plus yours. Interacting with one gives a random outcome: loot (Sporebucks), a free vehicle, or the tribe attacks your vehicle. The tribes that fight back become the competing nations.

### 3.3.2 Starting city type

| Tribal card | Starting city / nation type | Vehicle actions |
|---|---|---|
| Friendly | **Religious** | Convert cities; project holograms |
| Industrious | **Economic** | Trade routes; buy cities |
| Aggressive | **Military** | Bombard and capture |

The **city specialty** is separate from the **Civilization trait card** awarded at the end of the stage (see 3.3.13).

### 3.3.3 Money: spice geysers, factories, trade

- **Spice geysers.** About **16–18 per homeworld**, on land and at sea. A land or sea vehicle (never an air vehicle) right-clicks a geyser and deploys a **Spice Derrick Pack**, which starts producing Sporebucks.
- **Geyser output declines.** Every geyser starts at **§1,000 per minute** and falls over the stage to about §100 per minute (GameRevolution) or nothing (SporeWiki). Factories have to take over as the main income source. Early in the stage one geyser is worth about as much as several later on.
- **Capturing rival derricks.** Military vehicles destroy the derrick and redeploy their own. Religious vehicles convert the workers. Economic vehicles **"Bribe Workers"**, which costs money and a relationship penalty of up to −50. When a nation is destroyed, all its geysers become unclaimed.
- **Other income:** factory output, trade routes (economic vehicles earn money on each delivery, scaled by economic power), gifts from allies, and looting tribes. Selling a building returns half its cost (Gamepressure).
- **Spice Hoarder** achievement: control every spice node on the planet at the same time.
- Standard opening (SporeWiki, StrategyWiki, Sporebucks article): pause, buy as many fast vehicles as you can afford (usually about 3 extra), send each to a different geyser, and loot nearby tribes before rival nations appear.

### 3.3.4 Cities and the City Planner

Click a city to open the **City Planner**. It has five tabs: **Buildings, Vehicles, Decorations, Anthems and Outfits**. Building slots are fixed for each city layout. Military, religious and economic cities differ in layout and in the number of turret slots.

| Building | Civ cost | Role | HP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City Hall** | One per city, never bought | Centre of the city. Adds **+5 vehicle capacity**. Doubles production and happiness when linked to factories and entertainment, as a house does. Can't be destroyed in Civ. Military city halls have a missile launcher. | 3,600 |
| **House** | **§1,600** | Population: 15 citizens per house. Adds **+3 vehicle capacity**, for vehicles of its city's type only. Boosts linked factories and entertainment. | 800 |
| **Factory** | **§1,200** | Generates Sporebucks. Gives **−1 happiness** each. | 800 |
| **Entertainment** | **§800** | Gives **+1 happiness**. Defends against religious conversion. | 800 |
| **Turret** | **§2,000** | Perimeter defence with **600 HP**. Small cities hold up to **5**, large cities up to **8**. Each type looks different: military double-barrel, religious triple-barrel, economic cannon. Turrets don't count against the vehicle limit. | 600 |
| **Decorations** | **§25** each | Cosmetic. Can be sold for §25. | — |

**Adjacency links**, shown as green (good), white (neutral) and red (bad) connectors:

| Link | Effect |
|---|---|
| Factory–House, Factory–City Hall | **+production**. GameRevolution: "400 credits per minute for every house/city hall attached". |
| Entertainment–House, Entertainment–City Hall | **+1 happiness** per link |
| Entertainment–Factory | **−1 happiness** per link. Bad. |
| Any other pair | No effect |

Unlinked houses, factories and entertainment buildings don't function.

**Factory output (SporeWiki, Civ stage, per factory):**

| Houses linked | Output | With City Hall link |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | §0/min | §400/min |
| 1 | §400/min | §800/min |
| 2 | §800/min | §1,200/min |
| 3 | §1,200/min | §1,600/min |
| 4 | §1,600/min | §2,000/min |

**Happiness.** A city with **positive happiness periodically celebrates, which doubles income**. A very unhappy city **revolts, which halves production** and can lead to an uprising in which work stops. Unhappy cities are **much easier to convert religiously**, and religious AI nations target them first. Entertainment is a city's defence against conversion in the same way turrets defend against military attack. SporeWiki gives the maximum income of an optimised large city with at least one happiness level as roughly **§3,600 per minute for Military, §4,000–4,400 for Economic and §4,400–4,800 for Religious**. GameRevolution records up to 7,200 per minute from a single city.

**National Anthem** (StrategyWiki). Set the beat and instrument, place up to 16 notes, and add up to four ambient sounds. The anthem plays during Civ and is heard from your colonies in Space. *Ghetto Blaster* achievement: make 10 anthems.

### 3.3.5 Building Editor (Building Creator)

- Four sub-editors: **City Hall, House, Factory and Entertainment**. You design the City Hall first, at the start of the stage. When you capture a rival city, your City Hall design replaces theirs.
- Part categories: **Bodies, Roofs, Connectors, Doors, Windows**, building-specific parts (City Hall flags, satellites, trackers and domes; House lights and lawn ornaments; Entertainment lights, plants, signs and stacks; Factory smokestacks, gears and mills), plus generic **Details**. Most generic parts cost §500 in the editor; type-specific parts cost up to §7,000 (Metal Twister).
- **Building appearance is purely cosmetic.** Buildings have no functional parts or editable stats. Before Patch 5, buildings were the only creations that could be asymmetric without mods. Windows glow at night. The SporeWiki compares the controls to Google SketchUp.

### 3.3.6 Vehicles and the Vehicle Creator

| Domain | Unlock | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | Available from the start | The only vehicles that can claim land geysers. They run over creatures. |
| **Sea** | Requires a **coastal city or seaport** | The only vehicles that can claim sea geysers. Military ships fire homing missiles and have long range, so they can out-range turrets. |
| **Air** | Unlocked once you **own 4 cities** | Can't claim geysers. Cross any terrain. Military aircraft make strafing runs with a continuous laser. Expensive. |

- **Types.** Each domain comes in **Military, Economic and Religious** versions, decided by the specialty of the city that builds it. You can only build your own type until you capture a city of another type (see 3.3.10).
- **Vehicle limit.** Set by housing in cities of that specialty: **+3 per house and +5 per City Hall**. GameRevolution notes that houses only supply their own city's type, and that selling a vehicle refunds half its price.
- **Stats.** Each vehicle has **Health, Power and Speed**. Raising one stat's share lowers the others. Parts add to the percentages. Bodies and cockpits add Health and Speed. Wheels, propellers, wings and similar add Speed. Weapons, instruments and "economic" parts (flags, banners, crests) add **Military, Religious or Economic Power** at levels 1–4, costing §100–§400.

**Vehicle Creator conversion formulas** (SporeWiki; X = the stat's percentage; results are rounded down):

| Domain | Health | Power | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land | 150 + 6X (150–750) | 10 + 0.4X (10–50) | 5 + 0.4X (5–45) |
| Sea | 200 + 8X (200–1,000) | 15 + 0.6X (15–75) | 5 + 0.4X (5–45) |
| Air | 150 + 6X (150–750) | 10 + 0.4X (10–50) | 10 + 0.8X (10–90) |

Compared with land vehicles, ships get +33% Health and +50% Power, and aircraft get +100% Speed. SporeWiki: "for inexpensive power, invest in a powerful navy".

**Known exploit.** Switching a vehicle's design applies instantly to every vehicle of that type wherever it is, and also instantly reloads its attack. Players race to the geysers with all-speed designs, switch to all-power to take a defenceless city, and switch to health when enemies show up.

**Vehicle behaviour by type**
- **Military:** fires shells, missiles or lasers and **destroys** buildings. It can kill Epics and other vehicles.
- **Religious:** fires arcing energy beams with a harp sound that deal low **area** damage. They are weak against military vehicles but good at shutting down turrets and entertainment buildings. During a conversion, one vehicle **projects a giant hologram of your creature preaching**, and the city's citizens throw rocks and burning trash at it, so religious vehicles wear down over time. They can **charm Epic creatures** for a short while, shown by musical notes above the Epic.
- **Economic:** has no weapons. It runs trade routes and bribes derricks, and it is defenceless apart from what escorts it.

### 3.3.7 Capturing cities: the three methods

| Method | Who | Process | Consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Military takeover ("Capture")** | Military vehicles | Vehicles bombard turrets and buildings and then the City Hall. A takeover bar fills faster with more vehicles. When it is full, the city surrenders. | Nearly every building in the captured city is destroyed and must be rebuilt (the City Hall survives). Relationship: "You attacked us" starts at −30 and can reach −90. |
| **Religious takeover ("Convert")** | Religious vehicles | Vehicles disable entertainment buildings and turrets, then project the hologram. A conversion bar fills, faster if the city is unhappy. When full, the city converts. | **No buildings are destroyed** and there is no cost beyond the vehicles. "Your religion" penalty starts at −20 and can reach −60. |
| **Economic takeover ("Propose Trade Route" → "Buy City")** | Economic vehicles | The target nation must accept a trade route, which needs a relationship above +15 (or it must be the only other nation). Economic vehicles shuttle goods and a buyout bar fills. When full, you make an offer. | **About §16,000 almost always succeeds; small cities may sell for about §8,000** (GameRevolution says 8,000–12,000). Overbidding gives "You were generous" (up to +50). Underbidding gives "You were cheap" (up to −50) and resets the bar. Trade routes themselves give up to +50. |

- **Ad Blitz** fills one city's buyout bar instantly. **Gadget Bomb** and **Mighty Bomb** can capture a city by force.
- **Black Cloud** and **Static Bomb** disable defences.
- When two nations are at war and you trade with one of them, the other will demand that you stop.

### 3.3.8 Diplomacy and relations

Hover over a city to reach its actions: Contact, Give Gift, Convert, Propose Trade Route, Capture, Buy City. In the contact window you can **compliment, insult, gift**, ask allies to attack a target, or receive their requests. After a rival nation has 2 cities, it also contacts you itself.

**Relationship scale (Civ):** red, hostile or at war (−70 or below); orange (−69 to −30); yellow, neutral (−29 to +29); blue, friendly (+30 to +69); green, allied (+70 and above).

| Positive modifier | Value |
|---|---|
| Our Trade Route | up to +50 |
| You're a [same-type] Nation | up to +30 |
| You assisted us (agreed to attack, or stopped trading) | up to +60 (+20 if you refused before; +10 if you asked for money) |
| You fought our enemy | up to +70 |
| You were generous (city purchase) | up to +50 |
| Your compliments | +10 |
| Your gifts | up to +120 |

| Negative modifier | Value |
|---|---|
| Took over our spice derrick | up to −50 |
| **Used Nuclear Weapons** (Gadget Bomb or ICBM) | −90, capped at −100, **applied by every nation** |
| You're a [type] Nation (aggressive nations, Normal/Hard only) | −10/−20/−30 for Economic/Religious/Military if the judge is military; 0/−10/−20 if it is religious |
| **You are a threat to us** (2+ cities, not allied) | −40 / −60 / −80 for Economic / Religious / Military |
| You attacked us | −30, up to −90 |
| You broke a deal (stopped a trade route) | up to −40 |
| You declared war (at an ally's request) | −70 |
| You denied us | −20 the first time, −10 after, up to −50 |
| You were cheap | up to −50 |
| Your borders are too close | up to −100 |
| Your insults | −10 each, up to −30 |
| Your nation is too big (2+ cities) | up to −100 |
| Your religion | −20, up to −60 |

On **Hard**, the "too big" and "borders too close" penalties are severe, which makes unwanted wars likely. The in-game descriptions of the AI types are: Military nations "thrive on weapons and force… to destroy other nations"; Economic nations "use cash and trade routes… form alliances, but it's usually a better deal for them"; Religious nations "harness the power of words and ideas to convert other nations".

### 3.3.9 AI nation behaviour

- **Military** rivals can spawn as small or large cities and are generally the strongest. One of them usually grows into the planet's superpower and spams aircraft late in the stage.
- **Economic** rivals spawn only as small cities and are usually taken over quickly. They trade with other small cities but **never buy cities**. They have no defence apart from turrets.
- **Religious** rivals can spawn small or large and target unhappy cities.
- Nations on other continents are often busy fighting each other and seldom target you. The AI never tames Epics and usually doesn't route its vehicles around them.
- On Hard, one city on your continent often starts with a blue (friendly) face. On Normal there are usually two blue-faced nations, and the yellow-faced one tends to have the largest army.

### 3.3.10 Mixed ("cosmopolitan") civilizations

- When you take a city of a different specialty, you may **keep its original type or convert it to yours**. If you keep it, you can design and build that type's vehicles, but only in that city.
- The only way to unlock other vehicle types is to capture a city of that type.
- Officially a nation has only one specialty, and the History timeline shows which one counts.
- SporeWiki notes that capturing even one economic city with a single economic vehicle (for example one looted from a tribe) counts as an economic capture.

### 3.3.11 Civilization-stage abilities and superweapons

Abilities are bought with Sporebucks and launched from or near your cities. Each earlier card grants one. The final superweapon depends on your nation's type and needs **6 cities**.

| Source | Green | Blue | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cell card** | Herbivore → **Healing Aura**: an energy ball heals vehicles and buildings in a radius | Omnivore → **Static Bomb**: temporarily immobilizes vehicles, turrets and buildings | Carnivore → **Invulnerability**: makes your vehicles in a small radius invulnerable for a time |
| **Creature card** | Social → **Diplo Dervish**: sets relations with the target nation to blue for 90 seconds | Adaptable → **Bribe Bomb**: money rains on enemy vehicles in a radius and they attack each other | Predator → **Mighty Bomb**: damages or destroys buildings and vehicles in a radius, yours included. Can take cities. 600 damage to Epics. **§4,000** |
| **Tribal card** | Friendly → **Black Cloud**: shuts down a city's entertainment buildings and turrets, which speeds up conversions and military attacks | Industrious → **Ad Blitz**: a blimp instantly fills a city's buyout bar | Aggressive → **Gadget Bomb**: a single nuclear strike that can capture a city or derrick or kill an Epic. Requires **4 cities and §16,000**. Gives −90 with all nations and leaves radioactive junk in building slots for about 6–8 in-game hours, which carries over into Space. |
| **Superweapon (nation type, 6 cities)** | Religious → **Fanatical Uprising**: all remaining cities convert to you (they keep their city types) | Economic → **Global Merger**: blimps buy every remaining city, counted as economic takeovers | Military → **ICBM**: missiles from your City Hall destroy every other city's hall, vehicles and derricks. Earns the *Conclusion* achievement. |

**Superweapon cost.** Sources agree on **§12,000 per city you don't yet own**:
- GameRevolution: "costs 12000 credits per city that isn't yours, requires 6 cities".
- SporeWiki: the ICBM and Global Merger have a **§60,000 base** that drops as fewer cities remain (5 remaining × 12,000 = 60,000).
- A speedrun bought Fanatical Uprising for **§36,000** with 7 of 10 cities converted (3 remaining × 12,000).
- An ICBM fired while the game is paused does not launch and wastes the money.
- Players can also unlock the superweapons with the `unlocksuperweapons` console cheat.

### 3.3.12 Ways to win the stage

1. **Military takeover:** capture every city, or ICBM the rest.
2. **Religious takeover:** convert every city, or use Fanatical Uprising.
3. **Economic takeover:** buy every city, or use Global Merger.
4. **Diplomatic takeover:** once you own **at least 4 cities** and the world has no enemies left, allied nations offer to **merge** with you ("We've won! Let's band together…"). "Your nation is too big" can stall this.
5. **Surrender:** with 9 cities owned, the last enemy usually surrenders its city ("I think it's time to fight no more forever").

Epic creatures in Civ have **3,000 HP**, spit fireballs, and destroy vehicles, buildings and turrets with ease. Military vehicles, turrets and abilities can kill them; religious vehicles can charm them temporarily. Killing an Epic in Civ gives **no reward**. A wounded Epic retreats home to regenerate, and Epics sometimes swim between continents.

### 3.3.13 Ending the stage: Civilization card, spaceship and carry-over

**Civilization trait card.** It is decided by how you took **the majority** of your cities: military capture, religious conversion or economic purchase. Bomb abilities count as military. The card can differ from your starting specialty. SporeWiki notes that the Civ timeline is "reorganized, placing the strategy which they entered the phase from in the center", so in this stage green, blue and red are three separate playstyles rather than a spectrum. Getting the Economic card without having started Economic is "almost impossible", per SporeWiki.

| Civ card | Colour | Space ability |
|---|---|---|
| **Religious** | Green | **Green Keeper**: fewer biodisasters on your colonies |
| **Economic** | Blue | **Spice Savant**: more spice production from all colonies |
| **Military** | Red | **Pirate B Gone**: fewer pirate raids. GameRevolution calls it "Completely useless". |

**Spaceship.** Once every city is yours, you design a ship in the **Spaceship Creator**. It has all the Vehicle Creator parts plus a Space tab (grabbers, thrusters, landing gear); these are purely cosmetic. You launch it from your home city. *Starman* achievement: conquer all other cities and launch your space vessel.

**Carry-over.**
- Sporebucks don't carry over. SporeWiki says every player starts Space with §100,000.
- Players are advised to finish building, turret-defend and fill every city before launching. Turrets on the homeworld become multi-function Space turrets and cost §1,000 there.
- Decorations can be sold for §25 each.
- Your Civ cities become your homeworld cities.
- A military-captured planet keeps its damage. Gadget Bomb junk persists.

### 3.3.15 Civilization trivia and glitches

- The ICBM launch plays a "YEEEHAWWW" referencing Major Kong riding the bomb in *Dr. Strangelove*. The Gadget Bomb is named after "the Gadget", the first atomic bomb ever tested.
- Black Cloud rains Tamaranian Hoppers on the target city.
- Economic turrets are said to look like F.L.U.D.D. from *Super Mario Sunshine*. Crews fly out of destroyed vehicles, and the crews of sunk ships drown.
- The insult "Your people are descended from limbless space slugs" is ironic, because every nation is your own species.
- In the February 2008 demo, Sporebucks were called Simoleans; both use the § sign. A 2005 demo let cities build farms on their outskirts, but this was cut from the final game.
- Known glitches include invisible cities blocking completion, AI religious nations stuck mid-conversion, and the occasional bug that makes religious vehicles deal enormous area damage.

## 3.5 Definitive mapping: consequence cards → Space Stage archetype

The archetype depends on **how many green, blue and red cards** you earned across the four stages (Cell, Creature, Tribal, Civilization). For a full 4-card game, the order doesn't matter. There are 10 archetypes in 8 philosophies: Diplomat and Wanderer share *Order*, and Warrior and Knight share *Force*.

### Full game (4 cards)

| Card counts (G / B / R) | Archetype | Philosophy | Signature ability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4/0/0, 3/1/0, 3/0/1 | **Shaman** | Harmony | Return Ticket: teleports you to your home system |
| 0/4/0, 1/3/0, 0/3/1 | **Trader** | Prosperity | Cash Infusion: instantly fills a trade route's economic-takeover bar |
| 0/0/4, 1/0/3, 0/1/3 | **Warrior** | Force | Raider Rally: summons pirates to attack a planet |
| 2/2/0 | **Diplomat** | Order | Static Cling: stops all ships and turrets on a planet for about 2 minutes |
| 0/2/2 | **Scientist** | Science | Gravitation Wave: destroys every city and tribe on a planet (violates the Galactic Code, −30 with empires within 10 parsecs) |
| 2/0/2 | **Zealot** | Faith | Fanatical Frenzy: converts every colony on a planet (violates the Galactic Code) |
| 2/1/1 | **Ecologist** | Life | Safari Vacuum: abducts 2 of each species on the planet |
| 1/2/1 | **Bard** | Chance | Soothing Song: calms empires (relationship set to neutral for 2 minutes) |
| 1/1/2 | **Knight** | Force | Summon Mini-U: a small allied copy of your ship. Knights can't be NPC empires, and you can't switch to Knight through the "Teach me your ways" mission. |
| No cards (started in Space) | **Wanderer** | Order | None. The Species Shuffler ability was removed. |

### Games started at a later stage (fewer cards; SporeWiki "By card color" table)

| Start point | Cards | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Creature (3 cards) | 3 of one colour | Shaman / Trader / Warrior |
| | 2 + 1 of two colours | G+B → Diplomat; R+B → Scientist; G+R → Zealot |
| | One of each colour | **Order matters.** Listed paths: (Creature, Tribal, Civ) = B, R, G → **Shaman**; R, G, B → **Trader**; G, B, R → **Warrior** |
| Tribal (2 cards) | 2 of one colour | Shaman / Trader / Warrior |
| | G + B / R + B / G + R | Diplomat / Scientist / Zealot |
| Civilization (1 card) | G / B / R | Shaman / Trader / Warrior |

SporeWiki counts 256 possible card paths: 1 leads to Wanderer, 12 each to Bard, Ecologist and Knight, 31 each to Shaman, Trader and Warrior, and 42 each to Scientist, Zealot and Diplomat. In the Space Stage you can change archetype by completing an ally's "Teach me your ways" mission (except to Knight or Wanderer). Doing so earns the *Split Personality* achievement.

## 3.6 Source discrepancies

- **Tool hut cost:** 25 food (GameRevolution; speedrun budget) versus 20 food (SporeWiki Music and Fishing Spear pages).
- **Gift cost:** 10 food (SporeWiki, GameRevolution, speedrun) versus 15 food (Gamepressure).
- **Main hut HP:** 400 / 750 / 1,000 (SporeWiki) versus 500 / 750 / 1,000 (StrategyWiki).
- **Tool unlocks:** SporeWiki and StrategyWiki give the per-diet table in 3.1.6. GameRevolution simplifies it to "start with two, gain two each tribe".
- **Friendly card requirement:** SporeWiki's card page says all 5 tribes must be socialized. GameRevolution describes the card as depending on where the timeline trend ends, with Aggressive needing only to end "a little in the red". The trend-bar description is more widely supported.
- **Economic buyout price:** 8,000–12,000 (GameRevolution) versus "16,000 almost always" (SporeWiki).
- **Global Merger:** SporeWiki trivia mentions an implied per-city price of 15,000–16,000. The 12,000-per-remaining-city rule is the consistent figure across three sources.
- **Civ ability costs:** apart from Mighty Bomb (§4,000), Gadget Bomb (§16,000 and 4 cities) and the superweapons, no reliable per-ability Sporebuck costs, cooldowns or city requirements were found. StrategyWiki was readable through its API; the GameSpot and GameFAQs guides returned 403 errors.
