# 8. Lore, Science, Achievements, Trivia, Cultural Impact & Legacy

> Scope: this section covers the in-fiction lore of *Spore* (2008) and *Spore Galactic Adventures* (2009), how the game handles science, the full achievement list, Easter eggs and trivia, and the game's cultural footprint and legacy through 2026. Facts come from the sources listed at the end. Where sources disagree, the text says so. Lore details come mostly from the community-run Spore Wiki (spore.fandom.com), which quotes in-game text (artifact descriptions, dialogue and cutscene scripts). Its "trivia" claims of the "possibly a reference to…" kind are labelled as speculation.

---

## 8.1 Lore and narrative

*Spore* has no scripted campaign. Its "story" comes from environmental text: artifact descriptions ("rares"), empire dialogue, the Galactic Code, badge text, and one scripted cutscene at the galactic core. Most of it sits in the Space Stage.

### 8.1.1 The galaxy

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | The "Spore Galaxy" (usually just "the Galaxy") |
| Structure | An unbarred spiral with five arms around the **Galactic Core**, a supermassive black hole. It also holds other black holes (each linked as a wormhole), nebulae and proto-planetary disks. |
| Size (Spore Wiki) | **42,010 visitable stars**, each with up to 8 planets (5 terrestrial + 3 gas giants). About 150,000 planets in total, or about 120,000 terrestrial. |
| Size (Wikipedia) | "around 500,000 planets… orbiting around 100,000 stars". The two sources disagree. The wiki's figure is a community count. Wikipedia's is a rounder pre-release-style figure. |
| Galactic nucleus | About 7,800 stars lie within about 100 parsecs of the centre, nearly 20% of all stars (Spore Wiki). |
| Currency | The Sporebuck (§). Typing "$" in the editors produces the § symbol, which resembles the Simoleon from *The Sims*/*SimCity*. |
| Will Wright on scale | "…there will be probably well over a hundred thousand… far more than a player could ever visit. Which is why there is no point to having another galaxy." |

The game's "Galactic Objects" are listed in the Sporepedia with short science blurbs. They are the Galactic Core, Black Hole, Proto-Planetary Disk, six binary-star types (Blue Giant/Yellow/Red Dwarf pairings) and "Talked to Steve" ("You've talked to Steve. Good for you…"). Apart from Steve, the descriptions are largely accurate (for example, the core consumes "as much as 1000 solar masses per year").

### 8.1.2 The Galactic Code

The Galactic Code is an unwritten agreement among space-faring empires. It forbids weapons of mass destruction against other empires. The wiki compares it to the Geneva Conventions and the Outer Space Treaty. AI empires never break it.

- **Code-breaking weapons:** **Gravitation Wave** (Scientist superpower; destroys all buildings and sapient life on a planet), **Fanatical Frenzy** (Zealot superpower; instantly takes over a colony), and the **Planet Buster** (buyable with Conqueror 5 or Colonist 5 badges; permanently destroys a planet). The Planet Buster's description reads "Destroys an entire planet. Feared by all. BIG BADDA BOOM", a line from *The Fifth Element*.
- **Penalty:** every empire within **10 parsecs** suffers a negative relation hit, capped at **−200** after repeated infractions. Allies are hit as well.
- **The Grox exception:** the Grox *admire* code-breaking. Using a WMD near them raises their opinion of the player by up to **+50**. This is the standard route to allying with them.
- Terraforming a planet into uninhabitability and the Diplomat's Static Cling do **not** break the Code.
- A Civilization-Stage analogue exists. Using the Gadget Bomb on a city drops relations with all other cities by 90 "for using nuclear weapons". The Gadget Bomb is named after the first atomic bomb.

### 8.1.3 The Grox

The **Grox** are the main antagonists of the Space Stage and the only fixed, hand-made empire in the galaxy.

- **Territory:** about **2,400 star systems** in a ring roughly 2,000 parsecs around the Galactic Core. This is the largest non-player empire. A fan observation notes that 2400 reversed is "0042".
- **Biology:** a cybernetic species that can live **only on T0 (uninhabitable) planets**. On a T0 world they build up to 3 cities with buildings comparable to a normal T3 colony. A Grox abducted into the cargo hold is destroyed as "too toxic for your Cargo Hold".
- **Temperament:** innate relation modifier of **−70**, compared with −15 or −30 for Warrior empires. Trade is impossible: the trade button stays greyed out, apart from a rare glitch. The manual says only to "beware the Grox, as they do not understand relationships."
- **Unique assets:** ten asymmetric creature parts that are not available to players. Before Patch 1.05, asymmetry could not be built in the editors. They also have unique buildings (Grox Platz, Grox Sweet Grox, Grox-o-Plexx, Grox Stacks), vehicles (Grox Grumbler, Oceanic Grox, Groxoplane) and a ship with about 2,000 to 3,600 HP depending on role and 150 DPS. They have a unique, non-procedural anthem and a "glitched" machine voice set.
- **Missions:** Grox missions pay **double** (for example, §150,000 for an eradication mission instead of §75,000).
- **Alliance:** allying with the Grox drops relations with **every** other empire by **−200**, which usually means galaxy-wide war. It earns the "Dance with the Devil" badge. Destroying the Grox earns "Badge Outta Heck".
- **Unreachable systems:** some Grox systems near the core were originally out of reach because travel range shrinks near the core. Maxis released an "Expanded Travel Radius Near Galactic Core" mod package, and Patch 1.04 addressed the problem. Wikipedia says eradicating the Grox "yields an achievement". The wiki's achievement tables have no such achievement; the reward is the "Badge Outta Heck" badge.
- **Development name:** "**Grob**", "Borg" spelled backwards. The Grox are widely read as a Borg (*Star Trek*) homage, with a mechanical eye replaced by a red light. In war declarations they sometimes say "Resistance is futile!" or "EXTERMINATE! EXTERMINATE!" (*Doctor Who*'s Daleks).
- **Early sightings:** crashed Grox ships can appear on the player's world in the Creature Stage. Abducting UFOs in the Creature and Tribal Stages are most often Grox ships, which implies they are at least as old as the player's species.

Sample Grox dialogue from the in-game text: *"You've entered our security zone. We've eradicated entire species for less."* / *"Phzzzzzp. Scanning…Scanning…Entity identified… Likes/Dislikes: IRRELEVANT! IRRELEVANT!"* / *"Bzzzzzt. Object identified… Designation: Banal. Thank you for visiting. End."*

**Origin of the Grox in the game's own texts.** The game never explains where the Grox came from. Each philosophy's artifact set gives its own view:

| Artifact set (philosophy) | What it says about the Grox and the core |
|---|---|
| **Books of Science** (Scientist) | "The Grox are an experiment that has not yet run its course." / "a machine race that guard the center of the galaxy against all life-forms. It is not known whether their ultimate objective is to prevent us from breaking in, or to prevent something else from breaking out." / "…the motivation for the Grox's actions may lie in the difference between machines and organic life." |
| **Stones of Life** (Ecologist) | "The Grox are the ultimate evil in the galaxy. They are machines that exist entirely outside of nature." / "…a killing zone around the center of the galaxy. No one may enter this zone and live." / "…the center of the galaxy may contain vital clues to the origin of the Grox." |

Each set has 10 volumes worth §45,000 each, or double to the matching philosophy. A full set is nominally worth §4,500,000.

Archetype attitudes to the Grox, per the wiki's summary of dialogue: Scientists treat them as a neutral curiosity. Diplomats see a potentially powerful ally. Bards find them "no fun at all". Warriors and Knights see the "ultimate challenge". Traders see enemies of prosperity. Zealots, Shamans and Ecologists are openly hostile.

### 8.1.4 The Galactic Core, Steve and the Staff of Life

The **Galactic Core** is a black hole with polar jets. Ship range shrinks steadily as the player approaches. Reaching it is generally treated as "beating" *Spore*, although the game never ends.

The ending cutscene runs in two parts:
1. **The voice in the vortex.** A deep voice congratulates the player: *"…You have traveled very far and overcome many obstacles… The universe you inhabit is but one of many countless worlds, unseen but yet connected. Your creative efforts have not gone unnoticed. Indeed they have spilled into these other, unseen worlds just as your world has been enriched by them. It is now time for you to join us — Join us! Mwahahahaha!! No, just kidding. It's not scary…"* Llamas line the wormhole tunnel, a recurring Maxis joke. The wiki links the tunnel to *2001: A Space Odyssey*'s Star Gate sequence.
2. **Steve.** A small UFO appears with a squeaky voice and game-show music: *"I'm Steve! You are now to be given the power… The power to create and spread life, intelligence and understanding throughout the cosmos… so that we may one day become AS ONE… that is… I mean… in a metaphorical sense."* He then pitches "a wonderful time-share opportunity" with "a free breakfast and short presentation" near "the third rock from Sol", meaning Earth.

Facts about Steve:
- His ship copies the UFO on the ***SimCity 2000*** box art, apart from a loudspeaker on top. The *SimCity 2000 Special Edition* intro also shows that UFO flying into a galaxy's centre.
- He is the only character who speaks the player's language. In most localisations he speaks a Simlish-like language instead.
- Meeting him adds the 10th Galactic Object. He can only be met once per save.
- *Galactic Adventures* puts a Steve statue in **Adventure Town**'s museum. Its caption says he was last seen selling timeshares near the core. The statue also appears in the Maxis adventures "Concert in the Park", where it is described as the planet's founder, and "Bahaha 500 Time Trials".
- The wiki reports that an empire's history page records reaching the core as having "attained enlightenment".
- The music cue is reported as "Keep On Smiling" (wiki claim).

**What the ending means.** The voice's lines about creations "spilled into these other, unseen worlds" describe *Spore*'s online pollination system. Player creations upload to the Sporepedia and populate other players' galaxies. The wiki reads it this way. The game's metaphysics resolve as a joke in which the god at the centre is a timeshare salesman. In the wiki's reading, this matches the game's undercutting, comic tone about creation myths.

**The Staff of Life** (pre-release name "**Genesis Device**", after *Star Trek II*/*III*):
- A Planet Atmospheric ship tool that instantly terraforms a planet to **T3** and fills it with random Sporepedia creatures and plants.
- **42 uses**, a reference to *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*. It has a 2-minute cooldown and cannot be replenished.
- Its initials, **SOL**, are read by fans as another hint that it comes from Earth.
- The *Galactic Edition* handbook misprints it as the "Staff of Light".
- An Ecologist "collect" mission refers to a vanished civilization's "Genesis Project" that could turn barren worlds into stable ones.

### 8.1.5 Religion: Spode, the God That Will Come, and the Source

- **Spode** (from "Spore" + "God") is the deity of **Zealot** (Philosophy of Faith) empires. The Scrolls of Faith say Spode alone answers life's questions and that "all who reject Spode's Word" must be eliminated. Spode's enemies are the Grox and followers of the "False God That Will Come". Zealots speak of the "Eye of Spode", read as the Galactic Core, and Zealot delivery missions say Spode has tentacles. The Zealot GA adventure **"Temple of Spode"** shows Spode smiting "the Black Claw" in answer to Priest Falloha's prayer, which means Spode can act directly within the fiction.
- **The God That Will Come** is worshipped by some Shaman-aligned groups, and Zealots call it a demon (Scrolls of Faith, Book 9). The wiki reports cut dialogue ("space_diplomacy congratulations") in which, after reaching the core, Ecologists greet the player as "the God That Has Come" and Zealots accuse the player of "the final blasphemy by transforming yourself into the God That Will Come". This implies the ending once cast the player as that god. Shamans also mention allied orders, the "Savvy Zxxon Monks" and the "Teachers Who Are Still Loitering Around".
- **The Source** is the deity named by **religious nations in the Civilization Stage**: "The Source frowns upon you and your creations." / "The Source sings of our alliance and its power." Some localisations, such as Russian, just say "gods".

### 8.1.7 Other lore elements

- **Pirates** belong to no empire. They raid spice and artifacts and set fake artifact beacons. An invasion group is one mothership (250 HP) and five gunships (50 HP). When an empire is destroyed, its remaining ships are reclassified as pirates.
- **Epic creatures** are giant hostile creatures that appear from the Creature Stage onward, with epic cells in the Cell Stage. They have 1,000 HP in the Creature Stage, 3,000 in the Civilization Stage and 4,000 in the Space Stage. They are drawn mostly from carnivores, sometimes omnivores, and never herbivores. Their attacks include Stomp, Super Stomp, Fireball and Bite-and-Throw. In space, the Supersizer turns any creature into an Epic.
- **Lost empires:** artifacts mention empires from earlier builds, the **Greevil**, **Screeble** and **Willosaur**. A Greevil attack on a Screeble homeworld was shown at E3 2006.
- **The Monolith** is a socialization tool that uplifts a planet's species to the next stage. It is a *2001* homage with a precedent in SimEarth. Uplifting gives +50 relations, and 20 uses earns the Civil Engineer achievement.
- **The opening cinematic** shows a comet breaking up over an ocean, and the player's cell swims out of a fragment. The wiki and Wikipedia both describe this as **panspermia**. Further "meteor shards" in the Cell Stage hold new cell parts.

### 8.1.8 Earth and the Sol system

- Sol sits at **angle 225.06°, distance 7,295.43 pc** and contains Mercury through Neptune, plus the Moon, Ganymede and Titan. There is no Pluto or Kuiper Belt, and the asteroid belt was added in a patch.
- **Earth is a T1 planet** with an uninhabited, Earth-shaped landmass. A Maxis adventure template describes its terrain as "designed using USGS topographic maps". Only the player can colonize Sol.
- Achievements: **Manifest Destiny** (find Earth) and **Oh the Humanity!** (hidden; destroy Earth with a Planet Buster).
- Earth's presence in the game first became public when Will Wright used a console command to warp to Earth during a **demonstration at NASA** (see 8.2.4).
- Mars includes Olympus Mons, the Tharsis Montes and Valles Marineris.

## 8.2 Science

### 8.2.1 Design intent: Drake, *Powers of Ten*, SETI, astrobiology

- Wright cited the **Drake equation** and the Eameses' 1977 film ***Powers of Ten*** as inspirations, as reported in *The New Yorker*'s 2006 profile. Development began around 2000. Wright told CNET that the earliest concept, inspired by **SETI**, "was sort of a toy galaxy you could fly around and explore." The original working title was *SimEverything*. "Spore" was suggested by Maxis artist Ocean Quigley.
- In-game, first contact can trigger "**Drake's Equation was right!**", and the Space Stage includes a SETI tool. Wikipedia notes that the Space Stage adopts the **principle of mediocrity**: life is common throughout the galaxy.
- Wright told the *New York Times* (Carl Zimmer) that early work was inspired by **Richard Dawkins** and **E. O. Wilson**. He said the cooperation/competition "balance is driving the organizational complexity," and called imperfect science games "manure to seed future scientists."
- **SETI and NASA ties:** NASA's astrobiology site says Wright "drew from the work of Jill Tarter over numerous visits to the SETI Institute." Tarter and Wright held a *Seed* magazine video "Salon" (posted September 2008). At E3 2008 Wright announced a partnership with SETI. *Spore* betas were playable at the SETI Institute's "Celebrating Science 2008" on **July 16, 2008**. The **NASA Astrobiology Institute** co-ran a *Spore* exhibit with hands-on demos at **Yuri's Night Bay Area 2008**, NASA Ames, **April 12, 2008**. In February 2008 Wright demonstrated *Spore* to an audience at **NASA Ames Research Center**. GameStar reported a space-stage terraforming demo at the NASA Ames Virtual Worlds conference. His GDC 2006 keynote included astrobiology segments.
- **Terraforming model:** habitability depends only on **atmosphere and temperature** and is plotted on a ring graph. It is scored T0 to T3, and each tier supports more plant and animal slots and more colonies: T1 has 1 city, T2 has 2, T3 has 3, and homeworlds up to 10. The tools have "momentum": the plotted dot keeps drifting after a tool stops. The wiki notes that adding atmosphere to Earth floods it and removing atmosphere browns it, which some read as climate allusions.

### 8.2.2 How *Spore* represents evolution

Wikipedia's "Scientific accuracy" section lists the main divergences from evolutionary theory:
1. Species have **separate, not shared, ancestry**, and the player's lineage is **linear, not branching**.
2. Change is **directed toward intelligence**, while real evolution has no endpoint except extinction.
3. Change is driven by game stats and "**whatever the player thinks looks cool**", not by differential survival and reproduction.
4. Creatures acquire new organs by **collecting parts** from other creatures or skeletons. Real horizontal gene transfer moves genes, never mouths or limbs.
5. Cells grow by eating. The article contrasts this with the role of photosynthetic cyanobacteria in early life.

Critiques from scientists and educators:
- **T. Ryan Gregory** (University of Guelph): the game has almost none of the core ingredients of evolution, with no common descent, no variation within a species and no natural selection. "There are no consequences for dying, since you just reappear at your nest."
- **Richard Prum** (Yale): "The mechanism is severely messed up." He praised the game for raising big questions about complexity and contingency but warned that "There's no progressive arrow that dominates nature." **Thomas Near** (Yale) enjoyed it and hoped it would normalise evolution: "I think about it the same way I think about gravity." **Charles Ofria** (Michigan State, Avida co-creator) worried that it could confuse people. **Neil Shubin** (Chicago) liked that it shows evolution reusing a basic toolkit and was untroubled because "it's a game."
- **"Mr. Potato Head simulator":** Margaret Robertson used this phrase, crediting others, at GDC 2009. She said Spore "radically misrepresents anything that could be considered evolution" and was "actively misleading". She found no science teachers using it: "They don't exist… I've not found anyone using Spore to teach science." Teachers instead used the free Creature Creator for creative writing, 3D modelling, teamwork, and emotional-literacy work with autistic children. She praised the separately released **Spore prototypes** as "quick" and "scientifically accurate".
- ***Seed* magazine** (quoted in Wikipedia) reported that early concepts were more realistic, with realistic cell art and no faster-than-light travel. It said the shipped game "replaced [half its science] with systems and ideas that run the risk of being actively misleading" and that some scientists privately worried it furthered "the idea of intelligent design under the badge of science."
- **Development tension:** Wright described the team as split into a "Cute" camp and a "Science" camp, with the result "a very nice balance of the two factors." The cartoon eyes on cells were added "to make it cute," as he said at TED 2007.

### 8.2.3 *Science* magazine: "Flunking Spore" (October 2008)

*Science*'s "Gonzo Scientist" correspondent **John Bohannon** had a panel play and grade the game (*Science* 322(5901):531):

| Reviewer | Affiliation | Stages reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| T. Ryan Gregory | University of Guelph | Cell & Creature |
| Niles Eldredge | American Museum of Natural History | Cell & Creature |
| William Sims Bainbridge | U.S. National Science Foundation (sociologist) | Tribe & Civilization |
| Miles Smith | NASA | Space |

The panel graded **22 subjects**. Grades ranged from a single **A (galactic structure)** and **B+ (sociology)** to **F in mutation, sexual selection, natural selection, genetics and genetic drift**. One summary reports an overall **B** for the Space Stage's astrophysics, while the cell and creature stages failed on genetics and evolution. Bohannon judged that the game gets most biology "badly, needlessly, and often bizarrely wrong," and published a detailed online report card on a Science Guild wiki. A *Science* report also said some scientists interviewed for the National Geographic tie-in special had not been told of its ties to the commercial game.

### 8.2.4 The intelligent design debate

- **Wright:** "it's funny because in the game you're in the role of an intelligent designer… yet the meta-message of the game is that life becomes what it is through the process of evolution." He explained that an early prototype with automatic mutation "was not emotionally engaging", so the player became the designer. He described *Spore* as "toy-like and caricature-like". Wright identifies as an atheist. The team took care not to offend religious players, leaving "the whole creation of the universe question open." He said religious team members' concerns led to design changes, and that "Pretty much the only people who seem offended at this point are the really hardcore atheists" (A.V. Club, November 2008).
- **Intelligent design advocates:** **Michael Behe** said *Spore* "has nothing to do with real science or real evolution—neither Darwinian nor intelligent design." **Casey Luskin** of the Discovery Institute called it "a video game that is intelligently designed to allow users to create fantasy worlds where evolution really can take place."
- **Television:** On ***The Colbert Report*** (aired **December 4, 2006**), Wright discussed *Spore*, including a "surreal exchange regarding Spore and Intelligent Design". He described the game as "**a spreadsheet for God**", and blogger commentary called him one of the first game designers to appear on late-night TV.
- The *Chicago Tribune* (Eric Gwinn) suggested players avoid the debate by thinking of themselves as **homeobox genes**.

### 8.2.6 National Geographic: *How to Build a Better Being* (2008)

- An hour-long **National Geographic Channel** special that aired **Tuesday, September 9, 2008, 10 pm**. Wright announced it at E3 2008.
- It is a primer on "**toolkit genes**", mutation, evo-devo and genes shared with a 500-million-year-old worm. It is built around the Creature Creator, with Wright visiting the Field Museum in Chicago. UC Berkeley geneticist **Michael Levine** shows a fruit fly with legs where its antennae should be.
- National Geographic said it approached EA, and that EA was not directly involved in writing or production. The DVD is included in ***Spore: Galactic Edition***, alongside a *Making of Spore* DVD, *The Art of Spore* mini-book, a poster and a 100-page Prima "Galactic Handbook".

## 8.3 Achievements

According to the Spore Wiki, *Spore* has **100 achievements**, not counting removed ones, and *Galactic Adventures* adds **50 more**. The wiki's tables, reproduced below, list 101 base entries and 49 expansion entries, so the headline numbers are approximate. Some cheats disable achievements, and a known bug can block achievements in all future games after cheating. **Pathological Cheater** is the only achievement earned *by* cheating. Progress is stored in `pollination.package`, and deleting it resets progress. Each achievement icon's background colour shows its stage, and most icon creatures have three eyes. Names sometimes requested, such as "Omnivore", "Gone Shopping", "Wonder Wanderer" and "Supreme Pacifist", **do not exist** in the official list.

## 8.4 Easter eggs and references

Facts from the game's own content, collected by the Spore Wiki. Items the wiki marks as speculation are labelled "said to".

- **Spinning the galaxy** very fast on the main menu shows photos of the Maxis team, and Ctrl+Shift brings up more. In the Creature Creator, **Will Wright's head** appears instead. Wright's head and a Sims **plumbob** also appear in speech bubbles.
- **Steve's UFO** comes from the *SimCity 2000* box art. **Llamas** appear in the core wormhole, in artifacts, on llama-shaped Moai statues, and as the Golden Llama in "Ruins of Doom". The wiki links them to *Monty Python and the Holy Grail* and to Maxis tradition.
- **Music:** the *SimCity 4* track "Gritty City" plays over military cities. *The Urbz* track "Wiseguys" is said to play over some economic cities. The ***M.U.L.E.*** theme plays in trade view and is also in the GA music library. GA's "Pump-U-Up" is "Studio Town" from *The Sims: Superstar*.
- ***2001* references:** the end-of-Creature-Stage cutscene of a creature striking with a stick, the Monolith, and the Star-Gate-like core tunnel.
- ***Hitchhiker's Guide*:** 42 Staff of Life uses, the "42" achievement, Shaman missions about planets for "telephone sanitizers", and Book of Science 5's "digital timepieces".
- **Other lines and items:** "May the Spore be with you". "Why don't you make like an Aldrin and buzz off?" The Loyalty Booster looks like a **Dalek**. Vehicle parts named "More Cowbell", "Kessel Runner", "Star Deployer", "Goes to Eleven", "Glass Elevator" and "Flying Toaster". When the last nation surrenders: "Let's band together, so the world can live as one" / "I'd hoped someday you'd join us" (Lennon's "Imagine"). "It is time to fight no more forever" (Chief Joseph). The diplomatic option "Our people admire your wide tracts of land" (*Holy Grail*). A Bard conversion line mentions "cake", said to nod to *Portal*.
- **Numbers:** the wiki counts **626 Maxis-made wild creatures** and says this references Experiment 626 (Stitch).
- **Cross-franchise:** in *The Sims 2* Sims can play an old version of *Spore*. In *The Sims 3* they can paint Spore creatures. *SimCity* (2013) has a Spore-creature monster disaster.
- **Willosaur:** the three-legged creature Wright built in his GDC 2005 demo became an unofficial mascot. Fans named it after him.

## 8.5 Cultural impact and legacy

### 8.5.1 Pre-release hype and media moments

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Mar 11, 2005 | Wright reveals *Spore* in his GDC talk "The Future of Content", on procedural generation. Officially unveiled at E3 2005. |
| May 2006 | At E3 2006, *Wired* hosts a presentation by Will Wright "guest starring **Robin Williams**", with a private EA screening. 3D-printed figurines of designers' creatures were on display, and some E3 attendees later received 3D prints of creatures they made. Williams' public enthusiasm helped mainstream coverage. Reliable sources do not describe exactly what he did on stage. |
| Jun 26, 2006 | **Long Now Foundation** seminar "Playing with Time": Wright and **Brian Eno** on generative systems (Herbst Theater, San Francisco). Eno and Peter Chilvers later created *Spore*'s generative planet-editing music. |
| Nov 2006 | John Seabrook's *New Yorker* profile "Game Master". The NYT and *New York Times Magazine* also ran large previews. Steven Johnson framed the game as "the long zoom". |
| Dec 4, 2006 | *The Colbert Report* appearance ("a spreadsheet for God"). |
| Mar 2007 | **TED2007** talk "Spore, birth of a game": a fast live demo, showing five phases. Wright said he wanted a "toy" to inspire long-term thinking: "I think toys *can* change the world." The talk has about 1.5 million plays on ted.com. |
| 2007 | Wright becomes the first game designer to receive a **BAFTA Fellowship**. |
| Feb 2008 | EA CEO John Riccitiello at DICE calls *Spore* "probably the greatest creative risk… going on in the game industry today". |
| Jun 17–18, 2008 | The **Creature Creator** launches as a free trial and a $9.99 full version. Wright: "We hit 100K in 22 hours and a million by the end of the first week." By mid-July 2008, AFP reported that the creature count had passed the number of known species on Earth. |

### 8.5.2 Launch, sales and reception

- **Release:** September 4–7, 2008, by region (Australia and Europe first; North America September 7). Metacritic score **84%**.
- **Sales:** EA reported **more than 1 million** sold through at retail by September 24, 2008, across PC, Mac and DS. It reported "**nearly 2 million**" in the September quarter, and **over 2 million** by its May 5, 2009 fiscal-year release, which also cited users "generating more than 100 million creatures". Wikipedia, citing a different metric, likely US-only, says "406,000 copies within three weeks". No official lifetime total has been published. Figures around 2 million are the best-supported.
- **Piracy and DRM backlash:** about 2,016 of 2,216 Amazon ratings were one-star by September 14, 2008, mostly protesting SecuROM. TorrentFreak ranked *Spore* the **most-pirated game of 2008** at about **1.7 million** BitTorrent downloads. A DRM-free Steam version followed on December 22, 2008, and a GOG release in 2016.
- **Awards:** BAFTA Best Technical Achievement. AIAS (D.I.C.E.) "Outstanding Achievement in Gameplay Engineering", plus nominations for Computer Game of the Year and Outstanding Innovation.
- **Wright's defence of the casual focus:** "we would rather have the Metacritic and sales of *Sims 2* than the Metacritic and sales of *Half-Life*."
- **xkcd #480 "Spore"** (September 24, 2008): "Until I can afford 'Spore', I'm just playing through all my old games in order of scale." The alt text complains about lack of GMA 950 support on OS X.

### 8.5.3 Community, user content and "Sporn"

- The **Sporepedia** passed **100 million** creations by May 2009, and Know Your Meme reports **over 184 million** by July 2014. The *New York Times* reported more than three million player-built creatures submitted before launch to populate the game.
- **"Sporn":** within days of the Creature Creator's launch, sexually explicit creatures appeared. At about 350,000 total creations, EA was emailing warnings. Policy became warn, then suspend, then remove, and EA worked with **YouTube** to remove offending videos. One account flagged was that of *PC Gamer*'s Kristen Salvatore, over a creature she considered tame. The "Bad Baby!" achievement for having an asset banned was later removed.
- Community touchstones (Know Your Meme): the **Hug Monster** (EA forums, later an official forum emoticon), Maxis' **Clark and Stanley** adventures (widely parodied), the Willosaur, and Grox memes. r/Spore was created June 15, 2008.
- The official **Spore API** (REST/XML) ran a developer contest that concluded in April 2009.

### 8.5.4 Spin-offs, tie-ins and the film that never happened

- *Spore Creatures* and *Spore Hero Arena* (DS), *Spore Hero* (Wii, 2009), *Spore Origins* (mobile and iPhone), *Spore Islands* (Facebook), the Creepy & Cute and Bot Parts packs (a Dr Pepper promotion, 2010–11), *Galactic Adventures* (2009) and ***Darkspore*** (April 2011; shut down March 2016). *Spore Creature Keeper* was cancelled.
- A **CG film** from EA, 20th Century Fox and AIG was announced **October 1, 2009**, to be made by **Blue Sky Studios** with **Chris Wedge** directing. It stalled in development and is effectively cancelled since Blue Sky closed after Disney bought Fox.
- *Spore* was **Will Wright's last released game.** He left EA in **April 2009** for Stupid Fun Club, and his later projects (*Bar Karma*, *Proxi*/Gallium Studios, *VoxVerse*) are outside Maxis. Wright's career traces a line from *SimCity* (1989), *SimEarth* (1990) and *SimAnt* (1991) to *The Sims* (2000), each based on a systems source: Forrester, Lovelock's Gaia, and Wilson's *The Ants*. *Spore*'s sources were Drake and *Powers of Ten*. **Maxis Emeryville closed in March 2015**. Kotaku noted that *Spore*'s servers went down for maintenance four months later, in July 2015.

### 8.5.5 Retrospective re-evaluation

- **Kotaku (Chris Suellentrop, October 2015):** "Forget No Man's Sky, I'm getting hyped to play some Spore". He argued *Spore* was essentially *No Man's Sky* before that game's hype, likened it to *E.T.* as a memorable letdown, and called Wright one of the greatest designers of his era.
- **bit-tech (Rick Lane, "10 Years On: Spore", September 25, 2018):** He first remembered it as a hype-driven flop, but noted Steam reviews had turned "Very Positive" (about 14,060 reviews, 87%). He called the "five mini-games" charge partly fair. He praised the Cell Stage and the creation tools, found Tribe and Civilization the weakest, and called Space "ridiculously ambitious". Its galaxy predates *Elite Dangerous* by six years and *No Man's Sky* by eight. He ended on the game's optimism.
- **Game Informer (December 31, 2014):** Hello Games' Sean Murray discussed in a video how *Spore*'s reputation affected *No Man's Sky*'s reception. The two games were constantly compared as procedural-universe projects.
- **Will Wright (Axios, November 2022):** *Spore* "would have worked as multiple-interconnected games" and was "much more of a toy than a game." Axios called it one of the industry's most notorious misses relative to its hype.
- **"What Spore could have been" (2005 demo vs final):** In 2005 the game had seven phases: Cell, Underwater, Creature, Tribe, City, Civilization and Space. A DICE 2007 slide showed eight, adding Molecular and splitting Space into Terraform and Galactic. TED 2007 showed five. The shipped game dropped the gritty GDC 2005 look and the aquatic stage. A **Design Room oral history**, reported by *PC Gamer* and others in **June 2026**, quotes lead gameplay designer **Alex Hutchinson** saying the previews "built a fantasy in people's minds that was unachievable". Gameplay designer **Jenna Chalmers** said "Will said from the jump that this was going to be a lot of little minigames. Just nobody listened". Art director **Ocean Quigley** recalled a journalist calling the 2005 talk "either the most amazing game design of all time, or an act of bold chicanery". Wright himself: "We were definitely overrepresenting what it eventually became there." EA executive **Don Mattrick** reportedly warned against showing the game so early and was overruled. These quotes come from secondary reports of the oral history, not the original.

### 8.5.6 Influence and "Spore 2"

- No sequel has been made. Wikipedia describes "Spore 2" as a fan-driven idea, fed by the gap between the 2005 demo and the final game and by distrust of EA's priorities. It cites *Windows Central*'s Jez Corden on EA "wasting quality games… in the pursuit of profits".
- **Thrive** (Revolutionary Games Studio) is the best-known community "spiritual successor": open-source (Godot/C#), started in **2009**, and in Steam Early Access since **November 26, 2021**. Only the Microbe Stage is complete, with planned macroscopic, 3D, 4X and "Ascension" stages, and the team estimates about 120 months in Early Access. It is rated "Very Positive" (92% of about 1,700 reviews).
- Other games often named as *Spore*-like: ***The Sapling*, *The Universim*, *Planetary Life*, *Niche***. More loosely: ***No Man's Sky*, *Kerbal Space Program*, *Stellaris*** (per Wikipedia).
- **2024 official revival:** a post on the official website, the first news in years, announced **two new developers** and an **official Discord** (created September 12, 2024; about 15,800 members at listing). A GamesRadar+ report of **October 1, 2024** quoted the team: they did "not currently have any plans for major updates to the game itself at this time, or any kind of sequel", and their focus is "community engagement". An unofficial r/Spore megathread (September 30, 2024) reported that servers would be maintained for at least 3–5 years. This was a community post, not an EA statement.
- **Modding:** Dark Injection, the open-sourced **ModAPI Launcher Kit**, the DavoOnline forum and the Spore Modding Community Discord. Steam threads from December 2024 and May 2025 report that an EA update broke Launcher Kit installs.

## 8.6 Notable gaps

- No primary-source text for Bohannon's full 22-subject *Science* report card, which sat on a now-offline Science Guild wiki. Only the summary grades above are confirmed.
- No reliable account of exactly what Robin Williams did at the E3 2006 *Wired* event, or of the full Colbert exchange.
- No official lifetime sales total since EA's "over two million" (May 2009).
- No sourced evidence was found for "Avatar" planet names, "Ancient/Mother planets", or a "hidden galactic history" beyond the artifact texts and lost empires (Greevil, Screeble, Willosaur) described above. These may be fan concepts or mis-rememberings.
