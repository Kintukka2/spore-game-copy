# 6. Expansions, Parts Packs, Spin-offs, Merchandise and the Franchise at Large

> Scope: everything in the *Spore* franchise beyond the base PC/Mac game (Maxis / Electronic Arts, September 2008). This covers official add-ons, patches and editions, console, handheld, mobile and social spin-offs, cancelled projects, merchandise and other media, where the game is sold today and how it runs, the modding scene, EA's recent activity, and the games that followed in its footsteps. Where sources disagree, the conflict is stated and the better-supported figure comes first.

## 6.2 Spore Creature Creator (June 2008)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer / publisher | Maxis / Electronic Arts |
| Platforms | Windows, Mac OS X |
| Release | 17 June 2008 in North America (EA press release, Wikipedia) and 27 June 2008 in the EU (Wikipedia). The Spore Wiki gives 18 June. |
| Price | Full version: US$9.99 MSRP according to EA's launch release; Wikipedia and the Spore Wiki say $9.95. The trial was free. |
| Rating | ESRB E. The rating was granted in early March 2008, which hinted at a separate early release. |

**What it was.** A standalone version of *Spore*'s Creature Editor, sold as a "creativity toy box" in the run-up to the full game. Players built creatures, test-drove them in an animation sandbox, took pictures and videos, and uploaded them to the online **Sporepedia**. Other features:
- YouTube uploading in two clicks, plus a dedicated Spore YouTube channel.
- Animated avatars, and RSS and embeddable HTML for blogs and social networks.
- Creatures could be imported into the full game when it shipped.

According to Wikipedia, the editor was one of the first parts of *Spore* to get focused development and went through ten rewrites.

**Free Trial Edition.** This version contained **25% of the creature parts**. According to the Spore Wiki, the trial parts were the first two rows of carnivore mouths and the first column of every other category. It could be downloaded from spore.com and was bundled with *The SimCity Box*. The Spore Wiki also documents an **AT&T Edition**, a free download for AT&T customers with 37 more parts and paints than the normal trial. On 30 August 2008, three Australian News Limited newspapers offered the full version free or for A$2.

**Installation quirks.** Installing the full game replaces the Creature Creator. According to the Spore Wiki, the Creature Creator must be uninstalled first, and saved creations remain in `My Spore Creations\Creatures`. The SecuROM DRM included even in the free trial led to a class-action lawsuit filed on 14 October 2008.

**Upload milestones**
- By around 24 June 2008, over 1 million creatures had been uploaded (this claim is marked "citation needed" on Wikipedia).
- At E3 2008, Will Wright joked that players had made 1,589,000 species, the number of known species on Earth, in about 18 days.
- More than 3 million creatures existed by the full game's launch.

**"Sporn".** Soon after release, users built creatures with exaggerated genitalia or posed in sex acts. EA emailed creators of pornographic machinima, flagged accounts for terms-of-service violations, and YouTube removed videos.

**Reception**
- Metacritic: **83/100** across 7 critics.
- Eurogamer (Kieron Gillen): 9/10.
- PC Gamer UK: 90%.
- VideoGamer.com: 8/10.
- Common Sense Media: 4/5.

### Spore Creature Creator 2-D (September 2009)
On 16 September 2009, EA announced a free Flash version at spore2d.com. It featured:
- More than 250 body parts.
- Import of creatures from the Sporepedia, which held more than 120 million items at the time.
- A "Spore Creature Trainer" minigame.
- Embedding on social networks and e-cards.

Its senior producer was Caryl Shaw.

### "Sporelebrities" (September 2008)
On 2 September 2008, Maxis invited more than 50 celebrities to make creatures in the Creature Creator, and fans voted on them at sporevote.com. Participants included:
- Adam West, Stan Lee, Elijah Wood and David Lynch
- Brian Eno (a *Spore* composer)
- Katy Perry, Flight of the Conchords, Carlos Santana and Margaret Cho

## 6.4 Spore Galactic Adventures (June 2009)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer / publisher | Maxis (Emeryville) / EA |
| Working title | *Spore Space Adventures* (Spore Wiki) |
| Announced | 13 October 2008 as an untitled Space-stage expansion "next spring". Named in EA's 22 January 2009 slate. |
| Release | **North America 23 June 2009.** International 25 June 2009 (EA press release). UK 26 June 2009 (Wikipedia). The Spore Wiki says EU 26 June. |
| Platforms | Windows and Mac; PC and Mac shared one disc |
| Price / rating | **US$29.99**; ESRB **E10+**. The downloadable Robot Chicken adventures were rated **Teen**. |
| Requirement | The original *Spore* |
| Key credits | Producer **Kip Katsarelis** (lead producer), designer **Stone Librande**, programmer Benjamin Thompson, artists Michael A. Khoury and Ocean Quigley, writer Philip Campbell, composer **Kent Jolly** |

### What it adds
**Beaming down.** For the first time, the player's creature can leave its ship and walk on a planet in person. In the base game, a Hologram Scout is the only way to visit a planet's surface. Space-stage empires now hand out **adventure missions**. A flesh-and-blood captain can beam down only onto adventure planets; the Hologram Scout is still used on ordinary planets. According to the Spore Wiki:
- The game places an adventure planet in a nearby star system the player has not yet visited.
- If every nearby system has been visited, a gas giant is converted into the adventure planet.
- Adventures can also be launched directly from the main menu through "Quick Play".

**The Captain and Captain Outfitter**
- The **Captain** is the player's creature, kitted out as a playable avatar.
- The **Captain Outfitter** extends the Space outfitter with a "Captain" tab of weapons and gear.
- Sources give the number of new captain parts and abilities as **32** (Spore Wiki) or "more than 30" (Steam).
- Examples include the Plasma Pulser, Lightning Striker, Swarm Magnet, Jump Jet and Stunning Charm.

Gear is organised into **eight archetype tool-sets of four parts each**. Knight and Wanderer have no tool-set of their own.

| Tool-set | Focus | Parts (in unlock order) |
|---|---|---|
| Warrior | Raw damage at high energy cost | Bladed Knuckles, Plasma Pulser, Lightning Striker, Missile Flinger |
| Shaman | Damage over time and ailments | Toxic Crystal, Swarm Magnet, Icy Band, Hypnomelder |
| Scientist | Energy regeneration and capacity | Compact Generator, Compact Battery, Power Generator, Power Battery |
| Ecologist | Health regeneration and capacity | Invigorator, Stam-Booster, Super Invigorator, Mega Stam-Magnifier |
| Diplomat | Social abilities, no energy cost | Ducal Cape, Princely Pad, Regal Ring, Imperial Robe |
| Bard | Powered social abilities | Harpolizer, Hop Gogs, Synergizer, Fettipopper |
| Trader | Powered movement | Sprint Pack, Glider Pack, Stealth Helmet, and a fourth jump item |
| Zealot | (four further items) | — |

The fourth Trader item is cut off in the source page. The Zealot items were not captured.

**Progression ("points" and ranks)**
- **Experience.** Completing an adventure awards experience, which EA's marketing calls "experience to rank up their captain". Fans and the Spore Wiki call these **Captain Points**. There is no in-game currency literally called "Spore Points".
- **Unlocks.** Each rank-up lets the player unlock one tool, and a tool-set must be unlocked from left to right. Unlocking all **10** unlockable parts takes **4,375 points**.
- **Consequence ability.** Collecting the fourth part of a philosophy's tool-set grants that philosophy's **Consequence ability**, such as Gravitation Wave, in the saved Space game.
- **Maxis adventures.** According to the Spore Wiki, all Maxis-made missions, including the Robot Chicken ones, were later standardised at 100 points each, and replays earn nothing more.
- **Ranks.** Captains have **10 ranks per archetype**, each with its own titles:

| Archetype | First rank → final rank (selected titles) |
|---|---|
| Warrior | Thug → Fighter → Mercenary → … → General → Warlord |
| Bard | Wannabe → Traveling Musician → … → Colony Star → Galactic Star |
| Diplomat | Assistant Attaché → … → Ambassador → Supreme Ambassador |
| Ecologist | Oogie Keeper → … → Philosopher → Guru |
| Knight | Rogue → Apprentice → Squire → … → Noble → Lord |
| Scientist | Spaceflight Participant → … → Commander → Science Ambassador |
| Shaman | Gatherer → Harvester → Hunter → … → Spirit Talker |
| Trader | Intern → Analyst → … → CEO → Tycoon |
| Wanderer | Vagabond → Hitchhiker → … → Voyager → Intergalactic Traveler |
| Zealot | Believer → Worshiper → … → Prophet → Holy |

**Adventure Creator.** Players build missions by dragging content onto a pre-built planet. Available content includes:
- Creatures, buildings and vehicles from the Sporepedia (PNG cards can be dragged in).
- Fixed objects from earlier stages, gameplay objects such as bombs, teleporters, gates and jump pads, special effects, and sounds. New sound files cannot be imported.

Settings available to the creator, according to Wikipedia and the Spore Wiki:
- **Structure.** Up to **8 acts with up to 3 tasks each**. Adventure genres are Combat, Diplomacy, Collect, Defend, Puzzle, Quest, Story, No Genre and Template.
- **Behaviour and dialogue.** Per-unit behaviour can be Neutral, Peaceful, Mindless, Territorial, Aggressive or Advanced. Units have health and visibility settings, belong to one of four teams (Allied plus three enemy teams), can be scripted to perform actions on cue, and can speak in emotion-tagged speech bubbles.
- **Planet editing.** A **terraform / Planet Creator mode** offers temperature and atmosphere sliders, terrain stamps, colours, flora (including 60 new flora parts), and time of day.
- **Complexity.** A large **complexity meter** limits how much can be placed.
- **Captain rules.** An adventure can force a specific captain. Such adventures are shareable but playable only in Quick Play, and they are not "pollinated" into other players' Space games.
- **Exploration planets.** Players can build mission-free "museum" or zoo planets, which Maxis called "3D Sporecasts".

The expansion also added:
- Creation **lineage**, showing the original creator, the last editor and the current owner.
- Online leaderboards with medals and badges for record scores.
- Over 50 new achievements.
- A tutorial adventure, *Adventure Town* (the "Becoming a Space Captain" mission), set among the Maxis creature Bunny Froo Froo.

**Bundled and downloadable adventures**
- The box contained **16 Maxis adventures** according to EA's launch release (the Spore Wiki also says sixteen).
- **Ten Robot Chicken adventures** were a free download for every owner. Seth Green and Matt Senreich conceived the campaign, and the show's writers built it with the same Adventure Creator: Matt Beans, Doug Goldstein, Mike Fasolo, Breckin Meyer, Dan Milano, Tom Root, Kevin Shinick, Hugh Sterbakov and Zeb Wells. The missions included a quest for a library card from a "ruler-yielding librarian", demonic snowmen in a snow globe, exploding poo, angry yetis and banana wars. They were announced on 19 May 2009 and promoted at spore.com/wtf.

When the expansion launched, Lucy Bradshaw (VP/GM, Maxis) said the Sporepedia held "more than 105 million creations".

**Reception**

| Outlet | Score |
|---|---|
| Metacritic | **70/100** (32 critics) |
| GameSpot | 8.0/10 |
| IGN | 7.7/10 |
| GameSpy | 4/5 |
| Eurogamer | 7/10 |
| 1UP.com | B |
| MacLife | 3/5 |

Critics praised the Adventure Creator as "complex yet easy to use."

## 6.5 Spore Bot Parts Pack (Dr Pepper promotion, 2010–2011)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Content | **14 robotic ("mech") creature parts**, delivered as **Patch 1.06** |
| Announced / start | 4 January 2010 (spore.com) |
| Availability | Codes under specially marked **Dr Pepper** bottle caps, or with a large drink at participating **Taco Bell or Arby's** restaurants, redeemed on the Dr Pepper website |
| Region | United States only, excluding Maine. Wikipedia says it was later extended to Canadian residents. |
| Platform | Windows only. Not available for Mac, and contested for Steam users. |
| End | The Spore Wiki says the original deadline was 31 December 2010, later extended to the end of 2011 with no new codes issued. Wikipedia says "late 2011". |

**Controversy**
- The pack was limited to US buyers of a soft drink, and it contained the long-awaited Patch 1.06.
- Download links sometimes failed, and redeemed parts sometimes did not appear in game. Maxis posted a fix on 19 February 2010.
- Some bottles had no code, despite the "Every Bottle Wins" slogan.
- Pirated copies circulated, but they could not share creations that used the parts.

**Technical notes**
- **Installation order.** The supported order is Spore → (Creepy & Cute) → Galactic Adventures → Parts Pack. Installing Galactic Adventures after the pack breaks the game.
- **Silent mouths.** The bot mouths lack the Sing ability, which can crash socialising.
- **Later inclusion.** After the **March 2017** client update, the parts were included in the client for players who had redeemed codes. Other players still cannot get them.

The parts reappear on some "Cyber" heroes and enemies in *Darkspore*. Will Wright had wanted robot parts as early as 2005.

## 6.8 Handheld, console and mobile spin-offs

### Spore Creatures (Nintendo DS, 2008)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer | **Griptonite Games** (with Maxis). Wikipedia's developer-site link points to Foundation 9 Entertainment. |
| Designer | Dream Smith |
| Release | PAL 4 Sep 2008; NA 7 Sep 2008; JP 11 Sep 2008; KR 27 Sep 2008 |
| Ports | Windows Mobile 4 Dec 2009; BlackBerry 9 Dec 2009; iPhone 15 Jan 2010 (Wikipedia infobox) |

**Gameplay**
- A 2.5D story RPG based on the Creature Stage, with elements of *Nintendogs* and *Drawn to Life*. Creatures are 2D, while environments are 3D.
- The protagonist "Oogie" chases the alien Gar'skuther, who abducted Little Oogie and is splicing his tentacled genes into other species.
- Up to **280 parts** can be unlocked, starting from a budget of **20 Body Points**.
- Befriending works through cuddling and dancing minigames, combat is stylus-based with Bio-Powers, and there are **60 Badges** (achievements).
- Up to 31 creatures can be saved, and creatures can be traded over Nintendo Wi-Fi without Friend Codes. The online service closed on **30 June 2014**.

**Reception**
- Metacritic: **65/100** (DS, "mixed").
- IGN: 7.8. GameSpot: 6.5. GameSpy: 3.5/5. Eurogamer: 5/10. Famitsu: 29/40. EGM: C+. ONM: 66%.
- IGN nominated it for Best Simulation Game on DS in 2008.

**Mobile *Spore Creatures*.** Wikipedia and the Spore Wiki describe a *separate*, unrelated mobile game with the same name: a Creature-Stage sequel to *Spore Origins*. The Spore Wiki credits it to Babaroga and Tricky Software, released 17 December 2009. It has five zones with Epic-creature bosses, and it no longer runs on iOS 11 or later because it is 32-bit. Sources are muddled on which game the iPhone and BlackBerry dates refer to.

### Spore Origins ("Spore Mobile", 2008)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developers | **Babaroga** and **Tricky Software**, for EA (EA Mobile) |
| Release | iPod: 25 Aug 2008. A lockup bug on iPod classic led to a re-release on 31 Aug 2008. iPhone/iPod touch: September 2008, with EA's announcement on 5 Sep 2008. N-Gage: 19 May 2009. |
| Other platforms | Java/feature phones, Windows Mobile, LG Vu |
| Price | iPhone: US$9.99. The iPod version was briefly pulled and returned at $4.99 (Macworld). |

**Gameplay**
- A *flOw*-like Cell Stage game: survive as an organism in a tide pool, eat DNA and avoid predators.
- **18 levels on phones and iPod, 30 on iPhone/iPod touch.** Areas include Primordial Ooze, Volcanic Trench, Murky Water, Kelp Forest, Deep Blue and Sandy Beach.
- An editor appears every three levels, with Perception, Defense, Offense and Movement upgrades. The third upgrade in each category is a "super part": Lure, Shell, Electricity or Air Jet.
- Other modes are Survival and Arena. The iPhone version uses tilt controls and the iPod uses the click wheel.

**Reception and awards**
- Won **"Cellular Game of the Year"** at the 12th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards (D.I.C.E.).
- iMore: 4.5/5. AppSpy: 3/5 (2009).
- Metacritic lists it as "tbd" (too few reviews).
- It was delisted, and as a 32-bit app it does not run on iOS 11 or later (Spore Wiki).

### Spore Hero (Wii, 2009)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer | **EA Montreal** |
| Designer / composer | Jean-François Roy / **Winifred Phillips** |
| Announced | Hinted by Will Wright as early as a 26 Oct 2007 *Guardian* interview. Listed in EA's 22 Jan 2009 slate. Fully detailed 12 May 2009. |
| Release | NA **6 Oct 2009**; AU 8 Oct; UK and international 9 Oct |
| Price / rating | US$49.99 MSRP; ESRB E10+ |

**Gameplay**
- A story-driven 3D action-adventure built from the ground up for the Wii Remote. The team evaluated Wii MotionPlus, and its focus on the storyline came at the expense of online features.
- The hero hatches from a blue crystal meteor and fights **Zarkhator**, a villain born from a red meteor.
- Players collect **more than 250 parts** across five areas: Mushroom Valley, the Ancient Grounds hub, Moonlit Stoneway, Moonlit Caverns and Mushroom Grove, ending at Creature Beach.
- There are sing, dance and pose minigames, plus a multiplayer brawling mode.

**Reception**
- Metacritic: **61/100** (23 critics).
- IGN: 6.1/10. Game Informer: 7/10.

### Spore Hero Arena (Nintendo DS, 2009)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer | **Full Fat** (per Wikipedia) |
| Release | NA 6 Oct 2009; AU 8 Oct; UK 9 Oct |
| Price / rating | US$29.99 MSRP; ESRB E |

**Gameplay**
- A 3D arena brawler set in the same universe as *Spore Hero*. Players gather four blue shards per planet through missions, then challenge that planet's champion.
- Battle modes include Battle Royale (sumo-like ring-outs, with *Smash Bros.*-style knockback) and egg capture-the-flag.
- Each creature carries three Bio-Powers.
- Up to 50 creatures can be saved.
- Up to four players can play locally, or two over Wi-Fi. Online features closed **30 June 2014**.

**Reception**
- Metacritic: **49/100**.
- IGN: 3.8/10.

### Spore Islands (Facebook, 2009–2010)
- Announced by EA on **5 November 2009** as an open beta, and described as the franchise's first game built on Facebook Platform. Senior producer: Caryl Shaw. The Spore Wiki credits the design to **area/code**.
- **Gameplay.** A 2D "survival of the fittest" game. Players build a creature by swapping parts rather than dragging them, then place it on an island. They invite friends and send creatures to friends' islands to compete for dominance.
- **Stats and diet.** Creatures have four stat groups (Metabolism, Senses, Strength, Life) and four diets (meat and three plant colours).
- **DNA.** DNA accrues over time or can be bought, with accessory purchases and global and friends leaderboards.
- **Shutdown.** According to the Spore Wiki, it ran until **7 July 2010**. This is uncorroborated by press sources found.
- **Reception.** Gamezebo rated it 2/5 (Wikipedia).

### Darkspore (PC, 2011)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer / publisher | **Maxis Emeryville** / EA |
| Director / producer | Thomas Vu |
| Designers | Paul Sottosanti (hired from Wizards of the Coast) and Fred Dieckmann |
| Composer | **Junkie XL** |
| Announced | 20 July 2010, for an initial February 2011 launch that slipped |
| Beta | On Steam, March 2011 |
| Release | NA **26 April 2011**; EU 28 April 2011 (EA: "released worldwide from April 26") |
| Platform | Windows only |

**Concept**
- An online action RPG and the first Maxis game made after Will Wright's departure. Early design was inspired by *Magic: The Gathering* and the *Pokémon* trading card game, and the team numbered about 40.
- Players are the last **Crogenitor**, assembling squads of three "Living Weapon" heroes to purge the E-DNA-mutated Darkspore and their leader, the **Corruptor** (the exiled Xylan).
- There are three classes and five genetic hero types (Plasma, Bio, Quantum, Cyber and Necro).
- An enhanced Spore-derived **Hero Editor** offers "tens of thousands" of collectible parts and armour bought with DNA.
- Multiplayer includes four-player co-op and 1v1 or 2v2 PvP.
- An alternate reality game, *HelpEDNA*, ran in mid-2010.

**Online and shutdown**
- The game required an EA account and a persistent connection.
- In June 2013, login failures and a temporary removal from Steam raised fears of abandonment. EA said Maxis still supported the game, and it returned to Steam.
- Servers shut down **1 March 2016**, leaving the game unplayable because of its always-online DRM. Kotaku said this "revealed just how harmful DRM can be". TechRaptor called it tantamount to "banishing the title from gaming history".

**Reception**
- Metacritic: **65/100** (52 critics; Wikipedia: "mixed"). Contemporary reports quoted 75 shortly after launch.
- IGN: 7/10. Game Informer: 8.5/10. GameSpot: 7.5 (as quoted by GameBanshee).

**Revival efforts**
- A community local server, *darkspore-ls*, had reached menus and hero editing by July 2019.
- A revival Discord, "Resurrection Capsule", was referenced in 2024.
- No confirmed fully playable state was found.

## 6.9 Merchandise, books and other media

- **In-game "Spore Store"**: an iTunes-style storefront in the game linking to licensed merchandise, such as t-shirts and posters, and to future expansions.
- **Zazzle Spore Store** (zazzle.com/sporestore, from June 2008): players put their own creatures on mugs, t-shirts, magnets and stickers.
- **Spore Sculptures** (announced 18 Dec 2008): EA and **Z Corporation** offered full-colour 3D-printed figurines of player creatures, made on ZPrinters.
  - Ordered through **sporesculptor.com**, up to **4 inches** tall, **US$49.50**.
  - At the time, EA cited 40 million creations available as candidates.
  - Long, thin or spiny features did not print well (Venus Patrol).
  - Earlier, some E3 2006 attendees had received 3D-printed models of creatures they made.
  - The Z Corp release also mentions Spore **t-shirts, playing cards and comic books** as existing merchandise.
- **Spore Comic Creator**: revealed at San Diego Comic-Con on **24 July 2008**, using MashOn.com's e-card technology to turn players' "Spore stories" into comics.
- **Books**: the **Prima** Galactic Handbook (100 pages) and *The Art of Spore* came with the Galactic Edition (see 6.7). No dedicated "Sporepedia" print book or keychain could be confirmed.
- **Spore API**: public RESTful XML web services for player and creation data. The Spore API contest winners were announced in April 2009 (visualisations, games and mobile apps).
- **Film**: on **1–2 October 2009**, *Variety* reported that **20th Century Fox** was developing a CG-animated *Spore* film with EA.
  - Animation by **Blue Sky Studios**, directed by **Chris Wedge** (*Ice Age*), written by **Greg Erb and Jason Oremland**.
  - Produced by **Patrick O'Brien** (EA Entertainment) and **Lucy Bradshaw** (Maxis).
  - It stayed in development hell. After Disney acquired Fox and Blue Sky announced its closure, the project was effectively cancelled (Wikipedia).
  - Wikipedia also names "AIG" as a partner, which other sources do not corroborate.

## 6.11 Mac version history

- **Wrapper.** *Spore* for Mac was ported by **TransGaming** using its **Cider** translation layer. It was announced at Macworld Expo in January 2008 and shipped in the same box as the PC version on 7 September 2008. Cider titles required **Intel Macs**; Macworld reported that GMA 950-equipped Macs were unsupported.
- **Expansions.** Creepy & Cute and Galactic Adventures both shipped as PC/Mac releases; GA came on a shared disc. Patches reached the Mac later; Patch 1.05.1 arrived 4 March 2010. The DRM de-authorization tool was never released for Mac.
- **32-bit end of life.** The Mac build is 32-bit, so it **does not run on macOS 10.15 Catalina or later**. EA's community answers list macOS 10.9 Mavericks to 10.14 Mojave as supported and state there is no 64-bit version.
- **Workarounds.** AppleGamingWiki rates Rosetta 2 as unplayable, CrossOver and Wine as playable (with crashes after an hour or two), and Parallels as playing perfectly. Boot Camp and old macOS installs are other options.
- **Current stores.** Both current storefronts sell Windows builds only.

## 6.13 EA and Maxis activity, 2016–2026

- **2017.** A March 2017 client update. The "March 2017" executable became the standard modding base.
- **30 September 2024.** spore.com posted its first news in about five years. Game Rant says the team had been silent since 2019.
  - EA had onboarded **two new developers** and launched an **official Discord** as a community hub. Game Rant names the community managers as RogueLyeshal ("Rogue") and Reiliyn ("Rei").
  - The post was explicit: "we wanted to set some expectations right off the bat here, so you can take all those tinfoil hats off". The studio does "not currently have any plans for major updates to the game itself at this time, or any kind of sequel". The focus is community engagement, and the studio intends to "honor" the game's history.
  - Wikipedia notes that journalists judged a sequel "extremely unlikely, but not entirely impossible".
- **23 October 2024.** A Spore update renewed server certificates so online features (Sporepedia, sharing) keep working. It added no content. According to community reports:
  - Steam forced the update and changed the executables, adding new DRM. This broke **ModAPI SDK** DLL mods; `.package` mods were unaffected.
  - The Steam fix is to downgrade to the March 2017 depot: `download_depot 24720 24721 7407510787032991484`, then swap `SporeApp.exe`. Another option is to make the Steam manifests read-only to block the update.
  - GOG and Origin/EA-app players could skip the update, but lost server access if they did.
  - Rosalie241's **SporeFixOnline** backports the certificate change to the 2017 build.
  - Version strings discussed include **1.3.0.29** (new) versus 1.3.0.22. The Creepy & Cute installer ships its own older `SporeApp.exe`.
- **2025–2026.** No further content patch was found. The October 2025 GOG preservation work is a GOG initiative, not an EA patch.

## 6.14 Modding community

*Spore* reads mods as `.package` files, as in *The Sims 2/3*. Code-level mods arrived later through DLL injection. Modded creations can still be uploaded, but appear in other players' Sporepedia with a lock icon and cannot be viewed or edited by them.

### Tools

| Tool | Author / maintainer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SporeMaster** | Early community tool | Package packer/unpacker, `.prop`↔XML converter and RW4 model packer. Enabled the first custom parts; now obsolete. |
| **SporeModder FX** | **emd4600** (Spore-Community org) | The current standard. Java/JavaFX, GPL-3.0. Offers a text editor with autocomplete and diagnostics, a UI editor, and texture and model viewers. |
| **Spore ModAPI SDK** | Spore-Community (originally emd4600) | C++ library; mods compile to `.dll` files injected at runtime. Ships Ghidra data for the "March 2017" and disc executables. Documented at modapi-docs.sporecommunity.com. |
| **Spore ModAPI Launcher Kit** | Community contributors, MIT licence | Easy Installer (for `.sporemod` and `.package`), Easy Uninstaller with per-mod options, and a Launcher. Requires Spore + Galactic Adventures; supports disc, EA app, Steam and GOG. Latest version listed: **v1.5.13**. Not endorsed by EA or Maxis. |
| **Spore Mod Manager** | **Splitwirez** | .NET (C#/WPF) successor to the Launcher Kit, intended to replace it once finished. MIT licence. Its README says it is "UNDER CONSTRUCTION, and is not stable". Windows 7+ or Linux through WINE 6.0+. |
| **SporeModLoader** | **Rosalie241** | Minimal command-line loader and manager that injects DLLs before the game's entry point. Windows and Linux binaries. Rosalie241 also wrote SporeFixOnline. |
| **4GB Patch (NTCore)** | Third-party | Sets the Large-Address-Aware flag so 32-bit `SporeApp.exe` can use 4 GB of RAM instead of 2 GB. A widely recommended stability fix for crashes on modern PCs. |

## 6.15 "Spore 2", fan demand and Will Wright

- **Disappointment with the final design.** Wikipedia's "Proposed sequel" section ties fan demand for a "Spore 2" to the gap between the scientific, realistic GDC 2005 demo and the shipped game. Commentators blame EA's corporate priorities for the lack of a sequel. Press pieces kept the idea alive, for example Rock, Paper, Shotgun's "It's Time For Spore 2" (19 Oct 2016) and PCGamesN's "Forget Civilization 7, I want a new Spore game already" (24 Dec 2023).
- **Will Wright.** Wright left Maxis in April 2009 to found the Stupid Fun Club think tank. His later project, **Proxi** (see 6.16), is not a *Spore* successor. No direct, sourced statement from Wright about a "Spore 2" was found.
- **Design Room oral history (2026).** In a 2026 oral history reported by PC Gamer on 14 June 2026, team members said the early previews overpromised:
  - Lead gameplay designer **Alex Hutchinson** said the 2005 preview "built a fantasy in people's minds that was unachievable."
  - **Jenna Chalmers** said Wright had always described the game as "a lot of little minigames… nobody listened."
  - Wright acknowledged "we were definitely overrepresenting what it eventually became."
- **Official position (2024).** Maxis has no sequel plans (see 6.13).
