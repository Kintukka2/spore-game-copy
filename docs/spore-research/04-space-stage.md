# 4. The Space Stage

The Space Stage is the fifth and final stage of *Spore* (Maxis / Electronic Arts, 2008). It begins once the Civilization Stage is complete and has no end. The planet-bound nation becomes a spacefaring **empire**. The player no longer commands armies RTS-style but pilots a single, player-designed **spaceship** (the game also calls it a "UFO") through a procedurally generated spiral galaxy. From that ship the player handles exploration, trade, diplomacy, terraforming, colonization and interstellar war. It is by far the largest and most systemic stage. Wikipedia cites a 2008 Joystiq report that the space phase alone was expected to take **15–20 hours** and has a single scripted ending, while the four earlier stages together can take as little as 1–2 hours and up to about 15.

> **How to read this section.** Most of the numbers come from the community-maintained SporeWiki (spore.fandom.com) and from player FAQs, not from official EA documentation. Where sources disagree, the conflict is noted.

## 4.1 Entering the stage: what carries over

- **Spaceship Creator.** On entering the stage, the player designs a spaceship in the Spaceship Creator, which StrategyWiki describes as "one of the largest editors in the game." It can use all land, sea and air vehicle parts, every economic, military and religious part, and new spaceship parts. All parts are cosmetic and do not affect the ship's stats (SporeWiki).
- **Homeworld.** The homeworld keeps its state at the end of Civilization: its cities, buildings, turrets and vehicles. It starts with 10 colonies, or 9 if the game was started directly in Space. It is always a T3 planet with one moon, orbiting a yellow main-sequence star. The home system always contains a barren planet, a spiceless T1 planet with a crashed spaceship, a moonless gas giant, the homeworld and its moon. The homeworld cannot be terraformed or sculpted. It can be recoloured, and its creatures can be replaced only by using the Species Eradicator.
- **Money.** Civilization-stage cash does not carry over. Every game starts the Space Stage with **§100,000** (SporeWiki, GameRevolution FAQ). The cap is **§99,999,999**.
- **Archetype and consequence traits.** The four trait cards earned in the earlier stages decide the empire's **archetype** (philosophy) and its unique superpower, plus four passive "consequence abilities" for Space. See §4.9.
- **Starting directly in Space.** A player who has reached Space in any save can start a new game in Space, designing the creature, city hall and ship. That species becomes a **Wanderer** with no superpower.

### Difficulty differences (SporeWiki)

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base ship health | 1,500 | 1,000 | 300 |
| Spice sale prices | 25% higher | baseline | same as Normal |
| War attack frequency | decreased | — | increased |

## 4.2 Tutorial and main-line missions

The stage opens with a scripted chain of homeworld missions. The player holds the rank of Cadet during it and is promoted to Captain on finishing it.

1. **Fly through the orbs** hovering over 7 of your cities. This teaches flight with the arrow keys or right mouse button, and altitude with +/- or the mouse wheel.
2. **Scan a missing species** with the Scan tool to restore its Sporepedia record.
3. **Abduct an animal** with the Abduction Beam and drop it inside city walls.
4. **Eradicate 5 diseased animals** with the Mini-Laser.
5. **Fly to a nearby planet and scan a crashed spaceship.** This grants the **Interstellar Drive** and Proton Missiles. Then fly to another star and scan a ruined city. The scan sets off an alarm, and drones that mistake the player for the Grox attack.
6. **Place a colony** with a free Colony Incredi-Pak (§10,000 reward). Completing the chain awards the **Captain's Badge**.

Optional homeworld missions follow: find an alien empire, sell spice, find a rare, use planet-colouring tools, terraform a planet to T1, sell a rare, set up a trade route, destroy a rival's cities, and form an alliance. Rewards range from §5,000 to §50,000.

Later main-line missions are unlocked by **Master Badge** level (SporeWiki "Mission" page):

| Unlocked at | Mission |
|---|---|
| Commodore (2nd Master Badge) | Colonize 3 more star systems (default reward §50,000) |
| 4th Master Badge | Control 7 star systems |
| 5th Master Badge | Create 2 alliances |
| 7th Master Badge | **Contact the Grox** |
| After contacting the Grox | **Reach the centre of the galaxy** |

## 4.3 The galaxy

### Size and structure

- **Star count.** Sources disagree.
  - Wikipedia: "around 500,000 planets in the game's galaxy orbiting around 100,000 stars."
  - SporeWiki "Spore Galaxy" and "Star" pages: **42,010 visitable stars**, each with up to 8 planets (5 terrestrial and 3 gas giants). That gives about 150,000 planets, or about 120,000 terrestrial.
  - SporeWiki "Space Stage" page: "over 41,560 visitable stars."
  - In 2005 Will Wright said there would "probably [be] well over a hundred thousand… far more than a player could ever visit."
  - The ~42,000 figure counts only visitable systems. The ~100,000 figure appears to include all stars.
- **Shape.** An unbarred spiral galaxy with **five arms** around the Galactic Core, a supermassive black hole. SporeWiki says the galactic nucleus, within about 100 parsecs of the centre, holds about 7,800 stars, almost 20% of the total. Some stars lie off the main disc, beyond the reach of even Interstellar Drive 5.
- **Rules.** Stars are never closer than 1 parsec to each other, except in binaries. Every star has at least one planet. Clusters are common and are good for colonizing.
- **Coordinates.** Positions are polar coordinates from the galactic centre (angle and distance in parsecs), shown by hovering over the ship icon. The travel ring around the ship carries an arrow that always points at the core.
- **Decorations.** Nebulae, supernovae, pulsars and nearby galaxies are purely decorative and cannot be visited. Bright flashes, common early in the stage, usually mean an alien species has just launched a spacecraft.

### Star types

| Type | Notes (SporeWiki) |
|---|---|
| **Red dwarf** (M) | The most common star; small and reddish-orange |
| **Yellow star** (G) | Main-sequence star like Sol; every homeworld orbits one |
| **Blue giant** (O) | Large and blue-white; the rarest single star |

The game has no white dwarfs, brown dwarfs or supergiants as visitable stars, and stars never evolve.

### Rare galactic objects ("Galactic Objects" collection)

There are 10 entries. Visiting them counts toward the **Sightseer** badge.

| Object | Description |
|---|---|
| **Galactic Core** | The central supermassive black hole, guarded by the Grox |
| **Black hole** | Doubles as a wormhole. SporeWiki maps all **336** of them. |
| **Proto-Planetary Disk** | A young star with a dust disk and no planets. Rarer than black holes and of no gameplay use beyond the collection. A UI bug sometimes credits it as a "Black Hole". |
| **Binary OO / OM / OG / GG / GM / MM** | The six possible pairings of O (blue giant), G (yellow) and M (red dwarf). Their members are labelled "-A" and "-B". |
| **Talked to Steve** | The 10th entry, added after meeting Steve in the core |

### Black holes and wormholes

- Travelling through a black hole requires the **Wormhole Key**. Unlocked by Frequent Flyer 3 or Traveler 3. Costs §3,000,000 from your own empire or §1,500,000 from Diplomat, Scientist, Trader or Zealot empires.
- Without the key, a black hole does no damage but cannot be passed.
- Each black hole connects to a random partner. Black hole **locations are the same in every galaxy, but names and pairings differ per player**. Once traversed, a link persists across all saved games.
- To pass through, the player flies to the black hole and zooms in as if landing on a planet. The cutscene resembles a *Stargate* gate trip.
- Some black holes lead to "hubs" with 1–3 others within about 20 parsecs. The Travel Trail filter and the "Visited Systems" filter help map links. Players often rename black holes with the star-rename cheat to track them.
- The Galactic Core is also a black hole. It is the only one that leads to Steve.

### Interstellar travel

- Ship views are planet, solar system and galaxy. **Interplanetary Drive** is standard equipment. The **Interstellar Drive** comes from the crashed ship and has five levels:

| Drive | Range (pc) | Unlock | Price: own empire | Price: Diplomat / Scientist / Trader / Zealot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Start of stage | — | — |
| 2 | 5 | Frequent Flyer 2 or Gopher 1 | §150,000 | §75,000 |
| 3 | 8 | Frequent Flyer 3 or Gopher 2 | §300,000 | §150,000 |
| 4 | 12 | Frequent Flyer 4 or Gopher 3 | §750,000 | §375,000 |
| 5 | 20 | Frequent Flyer 5 or Gopher 4 | §2,000,000 | §1,000,000 |

- Each jump costs **energy**. Jumping with zero energy costs about **200 health per jump** (GameRevolution FAQ).
- Travel range shrinks as the ship nears the core because of "gravitational fluctuations." Near the core, Drive 5 reaches only about **3 parsecs**.
- SporeWiki estimates ship speed at an average of up to 5 pc/s and a peak of up to 10 pc/s on a maximum 20-pc jump of about 4 seconds.
- The **Speed Demon** consequence trait (blue Creature card) cuts interstellar flight time to two-thirds.

### Planets, orbits and spice colour

- Rocky planets, moons and gas giants. Orbit-trail colour shows temperature: **green** orbits are habitable-temperature, while red and blue orbits are too hot or too cold.
- Every rocky planet produces one spice colour. The colour depends on **star colour and orbit-trail colour** (SporeWiki probability table):

| Spice | Red star, green orbit | Red star, red/blue orbit | Yellow star, green orbit | Yellow star, red/blue orbit | Blue star, green orbit | Blue star, red/blue orbit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red | 96% | 50% | — | — | — | — |
| Yellow | — | 44% | 94% | 74% | — | — |
| Blue | — | — | — | — | 100% | 20% |
| Green | 2% | 2% | 2% | 22% | — | 30% |
| Pink | 2% | 2% | 2% | 2% | — | 35% |
| Purple | — | 2% | 2% | 2% | — | 15% |

So blue-giant systems with red or blue orbits are the main source of high-value spice.

- Unclaimed T0 planets have **hazards** that damage the ship (GameRevolution FAQ):
  - Cold planets: ice geysers
  - Hot planets: lava geysers
  - Thick atmospheres: thunderclouds
  - Airless planets: meteor storms
- **Storybook planets.** There are 10 official themed categories, with fixed T-scores:
  - Crab Legs, Craters, Dill Pickles, Gears n Cogs, Marshmallows and Pineapple are T3.
  - Pastry is T1.
  - Tentacles, Rattlesnake and Rock Stack are T2.

  Seven more datamined variants also exist. Storybook planets give no yellow signal and are logged by entering their atmosphere. They count toward the **Wonderland Wanderer** badge.

## 4.4 The spaceship: stats and tool categories

### Health and energy

The ship has a **health** bar and an **energy** bar. Energy-based tools (the top row of each tool tab) drain energy. Ammo-based ("charged") tools (the bottom row) do not use energy, but each charge must be bought, with a maximum of 99 of any one item. Health and energy are restored for a fee at any non-hostile system. Your own colonies charge less, and better relations lower the price.

| Health upgrade | Unlock | Easy / Normal / Hard | With Prime Specimen (×1.5) | Price (own) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | Start | 1,500 / 1,000 / 300 | 2,250 / 1,500 / ~450 | — |
| Small | Conqueror 1 or Colonist 1 | 3,000 / 2,000 / 600 | 4,500 / 3,000 / ~900 | §150,000 |
| Medium | Conqueror 2 or Colonist 2 | 4,500 / 3,000 / 900 | ~6,750 / 4,500 / ~1,350 | §300,000 |
| Large | Conqueror 3 or Colonist 3 | 6,000 / 4,000 / 1,200 | 9,000 / 6,000 / ~1,800 | §450,000 |
| Extreme | Conqueror 4 or Colonist 4 | 7,500 / 5,000 / 1,500 | 11,250 / 7,500 / 2,250 | §600,000 |

The figures are SporeWiki's. The wiki table lists Easy Medium-with-Prime-Specimen as 7,775, which looks like a typo for 6,750.

Energy storage has the same structure. Small, Medium, Large and Extreme Energy Storage are unlocked by Missionista 1–4 or Colonist 1–4. They cost §150k / 300k / 450k / 600k from your own empire, or half that from several archetypes. **Power Monger** (red Cell card) multiplies energy by 1.5.

**Cargo hold:** Basic (15 slots), Medium (20) and Large (25). Unlocked by Collector 1–3 or Merchant 2–4.

### Basic equipment

- **Scan.** Scans creatures, plants, vehicles and buildings into the Sporepedia.
- **Radar.** Beeps faster as the ship nears a target, such as a rare, a mission objective or a selected species.
- **SETI.** Hovering over stars in galaxy view shows blue "radio waves" and garbled audio where a spacefaring empire lives.
- **Abduction Beam and Drop Cargo.** Lift plants, animals, spice crates, money and artifacts. Hold the button to lower cargo gently: animals die if dropped from height.
- **Hologram Scout** (Missionista 5 or Trader 5). Beams down a controllable hologram of the creature.

### Galactic Code

The Galactic Code is an unwritten treaty that bans weapons of mass destruction. Three tools break it:

- **Planet Buster** (the only purchasable one)
- **Gravitation Wave** (Scientist)
- **Fanatical Frenzy** (Zealot)

Breaking it gives a "Broke Galactic Code" penalty of up to **−200** with every empire within about **10 parsecs**. SporeWiki's Fanatical Frenzy page says 10.5 pc, and the GameRevolution FAQ says 20 pc. Allies are hit too. The **Grox** are the exception: they *like* code-breaking on non-Grox worlds and award up to **+50**. AI empires never break the code. Rendering a planet uninhabitable by terraforming and using Static Cling do *not* break it.

## 4.5 Badges, Badge Points and rank progression

### How badges work

Badges are the Space Stage's progression system. Most of the 26 regular badges have **5 levels**. Each level awards **Badge Points**, from 1–2 at level 1 up to 5–25 at level 5 depending on the badge's scale, and usually **unlocks tools** for purchase. Unlocked tools still have to be bought. Your own empire always sells every unlocked tool, while other empires sell a subset, often cheaper.

## 4.6 Terraforming and the T-score

### T-score

A planet's habitability ("terrascore") is **T0, T1, T2 or T3**. It is set by **atmosphere** (density) and **temperature**. The terraforming panel shows a dot on a graph with three concentric rings, and tools push the dot with up to three arrows. The dot carries momentum, so players can overshoot.

| T-score | Ecosystem capacity (plant slots: shrub, small tree, large tree) | Colonies | Buildings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0** | No life | 1 city, shielded | City hall only, so no spice. The Grox are the exception: they live only on T0 and can build up to 3 full cities there. |
| **T1** | 1 of each plant size, 2 herbivores, 1 carnivore or omnivore | 1 | Up to 5 buildings plus turrets and vehicles |
| **T2** | 2 of each plant size, 4 herbivores, 2 predators | 2 | 11 buildings in total |
| **T3** | 3 of each plant size, 6 herbivores, 3 predators | 3 (homeworlds up to 10) | 27 buildings in total |

### Food-web order

Each tier must be filled in order: **plants first**, which stabilizes the dot, then **two herbivores**, then **one predator**. Omnivores fill whichever slot is needed. Each T-level needs a **different set of species**.

Planets terraformed without any life show yellow rings and are unstable: the dot drifts back. The Species Eradicator can open slots. Wild T3 planets are almost non-existent outside storybook worlds and homeworlds.

Community tricks:

- Drop herbivores before carnivores, or the predators will eat them.
- Plants and animals dropped on a fresh T1 world spread, so they can be farmed back up to 99 each (GameRevolution FAQ).

### Terraforming as a weapon and as diplomacy

- **Against cities.** Lowering a planet's T-score strips settlements. At T0 only one city remains, with no buildings. A Volcano dropped on a city destroys it outright. Heat and Refrigeration Rays do no direct damage. The Asteroid Call Button also deals physical damage.
- **Relations.** Raising another empire's T-score earns the "Helping our planet" bonus: +45 normally, +90 with Ecologists, up to +100. Lowering it earns "Hurting our planet," up to −100.
- **The Grox are reversed.** Raising a Grox world to **T1 wipes out its Grox colonies**, and the Grox treat it as an act of war.

## 4.7 Colonies, spice and the economy

### Founding and developing colonies

- A colony needs a planet **with spice geysers**, which excludes the crashed-ship world and the ruined-city world. It must be on **land** and near, but not on, a geyser.
- A system can't be colonized if any sapient species at Tribal level or higher lives in it. The player can wipe out that species, uplift it, or buy the system instead.
- The **Colony Planner** works like the Civilization city editor. Factories make spice, houses support factories, entertainment buildings keep happiness up, and turrets defend. Production depends on factory–house links. Low happiness halves spice output and then triggers an **uprising** that stops production ("Workers on planet X are overworked!").
- **Storage:**
  - 5 spice per colony, doubled by Spice Storage.
  - T3 non-homeworld: 3 cities, so 15 spice (30 with Storage).
  - Captured or bought homeworld with 10 cities: 50 spice (100 with Storage).
- **Production examples (SporeWiki):**
  - A standard 3-colony T3 layout gives about **396 spice per hour** (132 per colony) with +2 happiness each.
  - An optimized layout gives 108 per colony, or 162 with **Spice Savant**, with maximum happiness.
  - A maxed homeworld with Storage gives about **900 per hour**, filling in about 6.7 minutes.
- The player's **own homeworld produces only red spice, and slowly**. The GameRevolution FAQ puts it at about 10% of a full colony.
- Fully kitting out a 3-colony T3 planet costs about **§2.2–2.3 million**: three Incredi-Paks, Spice Storage, Uber Turret, Bio Protector, Bio Stabilizer, buildings and turrets.

### Spice: colours and prices

Six spice colours exist. A glitched **"white spice"**, which cannot be sold, can appear when an AI empire colonizes a gas giant. The cargo hold carries up to **99 units of each colour**.

**Normal/Hard prices (§ per unit, SporeWiki).** "Min" is the price on a planet that already produces that colour. "Max" is the highest possible sell price.

| | Red | Yellow | Blue | Green | Pink | Purple |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical buy price | 3,375 | 4,500 | 5,230 | 10,125 | 11,242 | 14,625 |
| Min sell | 225 | 300 | 349 | 675 | 749 | 975 |
| Max sell | 13,497 | 17,995 | 20,915 | 40,490 | 44,957 | 58,485 |
| Max sell on Easy (+25%) | 16,868 | 22,491 | 26,140 | 50,605 | 56,188 | **73,096** |

- **Pricing rules (SporeWiki):**
  - A colony always pays the minimum for its own colour.
  - Two more random colours are also bought at the minimum.
  - Each remaining colour gets a random price between 1× and 5× its base.
  - Prices re-roll on save/load, over time, or after visiting other empires.
- **Value tiers.** Red and yellow are cheapest, blue and green are mid-range, and pink and purple are the most valuable. SporeWiki's in-game descriptions call red "rare" even though it is the most common.
- Selling 99 of every colour at maximum price yields **§24,300,648 on Easy** or **§19,437,561 on Normal/Hard**.
- **Other ways to get spice:**
  - Buy it from friendly empires; the selling planet must be T1 or higher.
  - Receive it through trade routes.
  - **Steal** crates with the Abduction Beam. Stealing from empires costs "Stole our resources" relations. Stealing 50 crates earns the *Thief* achievement.
- **Pirates** sometimes loot 2–3 units. Abducting the crate yourself foils the raid.

### Trade routes, buying systems, wealth

- **Trade routes.** An empire must be at a **+30 or better** relationship to open one. Each route gives a +30 bonus, or +40 with Traders, while it lasts. It links the partner's system to your nearest system, delivers their spice to that system, and fills an **economic-takeover bar**.
- **Route limit.** Sources differ. SporeWiki says up to 5 active routes. The GameRevolution FAQ says 3. Patch 1.05 "increased limits on concurrent trade routes."
- **Buying a system.** When the takeover bar fills, the empire offers to sell the system. A failed bid resets the bar. SporeWiki's rule-of-thumb offers:

| Offer | Usually enough for |
|---|---|
| §0.5M | Systems with only a city hall |
| §1M | T0 systems |
| §3M | T1 systems |
| §5M | T2 systems |
| §10M | Always succeeds, even on homeworlds |

  The highest T-score planet in the system sets the price.
- **Trader shortcut.** The Trader's **Cash Infusion** fills the bar instantly.
- **Other income:**
  - Mission rewards, from §5,000 to over §1.2M. The Grox pay double.
  - Artifact sales, §22,500–225,000 each. A complete 10-piece set sells for 10× its individual value. Philosophy relics sell for double to their matching archetype.
  - Salvage from destroyed ships and cities.
  - Gifts from friendly empires.

### Colony threats

- **Eco-disasters.** A virus infects a species on one of your planets or an ally's planet, roughly every 20–30 minutes.
  - You must kill the **5 glowing infected creatures** within **3:00**. A Bio Protector extends this to 12 minutes.
  - If you fail, the species goes extinct and the infection spreads, this time without markers. If a whole food-web row is lost, the T-score drops and cities die.
  - Failing an ally costs −40 to −60 relations.
  - Patch 1.05 reduced disaster rates on all difficulties.
- **Pirates.** These are unaligned raiders: a mothership with 250 HP plus five 50-HP gunships.
  - They raid colonies or steal spice, and they set up fake artifact signals as ambushes.
  - When an empire is destroyed, its ships become pirates.
  - **Pirate B Gone** (red Civilization card) reduces raids.
- **Invasions.** At-war empires attack colonies inside their "danger radius." The Grox's radius covers the whole galaxy. An enemy that attacks your homeworld destroys every city but one if it isn't stopped.
- **Infected citizens.** Missions sometimes ask you to "eliminate the infected citizens" on an alien planet.
- **Epic creatures.** In Space they are rare, have up to **4,000 HP**, and shoot accurate fireballs at your ship. They can't be abducted. A Supersizer can create one, and players have exploited this to wreck rival cities without starting a war.

## 4.8 Empires, relations and diplomacy

### AI empires

- AI empires spawn at random. SETI shows them as blue pulses.
- Each starts with a multi-city homeworld, shown as a solid sphere on the map. Colonies show as rings.
- Each has a random colour and one of **8 archetypes**. Knight and Wanderer are player-only. Pollinated species do *not* keep their creator's archetype.
- **Strength factor** (the star rank shown on their systems) depends on the number of systems they hold:

| Level | Systems | Ship HP |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 | 300 |
| 2 | 4–5 | 450 |
| 3 | 6–8 | 750 |
| 4 | 9–12 | 1,200 |
| 5 | 13+ | 1,800 |

- Only level 4–5 empires offer **archetype-change missions**.
- **Behaviour by archetype:**
  - Diplomats and Traders expand fastest, often onto bare T0 worlds, and never attack the colonies of empires they are at war with.
  - Level-5 Scientist, Bard, Shaman and Ecologist empires terraform their worlds to T2.
- **"Awakening" (GameRevolution FAQ).** An empire is dormant until one of your saves enters its range. After that it expands whenever any Space save is played.
- **NPC fleets.** Patrol ships defend planets. Cruisers launch groups of 5 fighters and bombard cities. An allied empire asked to attack sends 20 ships. With 5 fleet allies and 20 Raider Rally pirates, a single battle can involve 45 or more ships.

### Relationship scale

Relations use the Civilization-stage bar:

| Face | Range |
|---|---|
| Red (enemy, at war) | −70 or below |
| Orange (unfriendly) | −69 to −30 |
| Yellow (neutral) | −29 to 29 |
| Blue (friendly) | 30 to 69 |
| Green (ally) | 70+ |

- Trading is only possible at a relationship above 0.
- Unfriendly empires demand **tribute**. Refusing costs up to −100.
- Peace can be bought. SporeWiki says this usually costs a few million on Easy and up to about 16 million on Normal.
- Most modifiers fade over time.

**First contact** offers three replies:

- "I come in peace": +10, permanent.
- "Behold! I am from the great and mighty planet…": +10, temporary.
- "Call off your fighter…": −10.

**Main positive modifiers (SporeWiki):**

| Your embassy | up to +50 |
| Your gifts | up to +50 |
| Using friendly tools | up to +50 |
| We created an alliance | up to +50 |

### Alliances

- An empire accepts an alliance at a green face.
- Allies supply **fleet ships**, whose strength depends on their empire's size. They will **attack targets** on request and can teach their philosophy.
- An alliance weakens at a blue face ("losing interest") and breaks at yellow.
- An **embassy plus a trade route** together hold about +80, enough to keep most alliances stable.
- Losing an allied ship costs relations.

### Missions from empires

Asking any planet for a mission gives one randomly generated task. Rejecting a mission after viewing it costs relations. Mission types:

- Destroy or capture all cities on a planet
- Destroy all turrets
- Fetch a coloured spice, animal, plant, citizen or tribal member (often under a 6–10 minute timer once on the planet)
- Balance an ecosystem
- Gather plant and animal samples
- Eradicate diseased creatures
- Eliminate infected citizens
- Find a treasured artifact, or deliver 3 artifacts
- Scan lifeforms

**Alert missions:**

- Eco-disaster
- "Hostile UFOs are attacking planet X!"
- "Hostile UFOs are pirating planet X!" (pirates). Warrior allies call this "Target practice."
- Overworked workers

## 4.9 Archetypes (philosophies) and superpowers

### How an archetype is assigned

The archetype comes from the colours of the four stage cards:

- Green = social/peaceful
- Blue = adaptable/economic
- Red = aggressive/military

There are **10 archetypes** and **8 philosophies**. Diplomat and Wanderer share the Philosophy of Order, and Warrior and Knight share the Philosophy of Force. SporeWiki counts 256 card paths in total: 1 to Wanderer, 12 each to Bard, Ecologist and Knight, 31 each to Shaman, Trader and Warrior, and 42 each to Scientist, Zealot and Diplomat. **Knight, Bard and Ecologist** need the game to have started in the Cell stage.

Superpowers cost no Sporebucks or spice. They are **free, permanent tools with a cooldown** instead. Saving and reloading resets every cooldown.

| Archetype (philosophy) | Cards | Superpower | Effect | Cooldown (SporeWiki "Cooldown" page) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shaman** (Harmony) | 3+ green | **Return Ticket** | Opens a portal home from anywhere | **1 min** (the shortest) |
| **Trader** (Prosperity) | 3+ blue | **Cash Infusion** | Instantly fills a system's economic-takeover bar where you have a trade route | 20 min |
| **Warrior** (Force) | 3+ red | **Raider Rally** | Opens a pirate portal that attacks the planet. Can't be used on your homeworld. | 20 min |
| **Zealot** (Faith) | red + green | **Fanatical Frenzy** | Converts every city on the planet to your empire intact. Breaks the Code (−100 nearby). Doesn't count for the Conqueror badge. | **30 min** (the longest) |
| **Scientist** (Science) | red + blue | **Gravitation Wave** | Destroys every city, building and tribe on a planet but leaves wildlife and T-score intact. Breaks the Code. | 20 min |
| **Diplomat** (Order) | blue + green | **Static Cling** | Freezes all ships and turrets on the planet | 20 min on the Cooldown page; the tool's own page says 23 min and a 3-minute effect, while the archetype page says 2 minutes |

### NPC personalities

- **Bard, Ecologist, Shaman and Scientist:** passive; start neutral; rarely demand tribute.
- **Diplomat and Trader:** friendly (+30 "new acquaintances"); expand fast.
- **Warrior:** −15 on Easy and −30 on Normal/Hard ("We distrust strangers"); demands tribute; picks fights.
- **Zealot:** aggressive and preaches about **Spode**; demands tribute even from fellow Zealots.
- In other saves, a Knight empire behaves as a Warrior and a Wanderer as a Diplomat.
- Each philosophy has lore told through its **relic set**: Blocks of Chance, Books of Science, Scrolls of Faith, Scrolls of Harmony, Scrolls of Order, Stones of Force, Stones of Life and Tablets of Prosperity.
- Archetypes differ in how they view the Grox. Scientists treat them as a curiosity, Diplomats as a potential ally, and Zealots as anathema.

### Archetype change ("drift")

There is no gradual drift. Change happens only through a mission. The player allies with a **strength-4 or strength-5** empire of the target archetype, chooses "Teach me your ways," pays, and completes the task. The old superpower is lost; the Diplomat empire says the Galactic Code forbids more than one. Changing earns the **Split Personality** badge.

| Target | Cost | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Bard | §10,000,000 | Collect 25 rare artifacts |
| Diplomat | §5,000,000 | Form 5 new alliances |
| Ecologist | §10,000,000 | Fill out 50 ecosystems |
| Scientist | §5,000,000 | Explore 20 non-planetary systems |
| Shaman | §2,500,000 | Paint or sculpt 150 planets |
| Trader | §2,500,000 | Sell 5,000 items |
| Warrior | §5,000,000 | Conquer 20 systems |
| Zealot | §5,000,000 | Found colonies on 15 new planets |

Knight and Wanderer can't be gained this way. A Knight or Wanderer save offers the Warrior or Diplomat mission instead.

These are passive bonuses for Space, one per stage card:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green | **Social Suave**: 20% off social tools | **Pleasing Performance**: happier colonies | **Gracious Greeting**: +10 relations | **Green Keeper**: fewer bio-disasters |
| Blue | **Gentle Generalist**: 20% off standard equipment | **Speed Demon**: two-thirds the flight time | **Colony Craze**: 20% off colony tools | **Spice Savant**: more spice |
| Red | **Power Monger**: ×1.5 energy | **Prime Specimen**: ×1.5 health | **Arms Dealer**: 20% off weapons | **Pirate B Gone**: fewer raids |

## 4.10 The Grox and the Galactic Core

### The Grox

- **Territory.** The only fixed, hand-made empire in the galaxy. It holds **2,400 systems** (about 5,000 planets) around the core. SporeWiki describes the area as about 2,000 parsecs in circumference.
- **Biology.** They are cybernetic and live **only on T0 planets**. Abducting one destroys it: it is "too toxic for your Cargo Hold." Terraforming a Grox world to T1 wipes out its colony.
- **Hostility.**
  - Permanent "We distrust strangers" of **−70**.
  - They attack anyone deep in their space unless formally allied, and their invasion radius covers the whole galaxy.
  - **They never accept peace** once at war.
  - They can't be traded with; the trade button is greyed out.
  - Their missions pay double, for example §150,000 instead of §75,000.
- **Ship HP by role (SporeWiki Grox page):**

| Role | HP |
|---|---|
| Escort | 400 |
| Defensive class 2 | 800 |
| Offensive | 2,000 |
| Defensive class 1 | 2,400 |
| Allied | 3,600 |

  They deal about 150 damage per shot and fly at about 66% of the player's speed. SporeWiki's Galactic Core page instead gives a single figure of 2,700 HP.
- **Assets.** Exclusive asymmetric creature parts, buildings (Grox Platz, Grox Sweet Grox, Grox-o-Plexx, Grox Stacks), vehicles (Grox Grumbler, Oceanic Grox, Groxoplane) and a unique, non-procedural **anthem**.
- **Name.** The internal beta name was "Grob," which is "Borg" reversed. Their war cries include "Resistance is futile!" and "EXTERMINATE!"
- **Patches.** Patch 1.04 (June 23, 2009) and 1.05 (July 22, 2009) made every Grox world reachable. Before that, *Badge Outta Heck* (destroy the Grox) was impossible.

### Allying with the Grox

This requires very careful relationship farming:

- Super Happy Ray, which gives up to +30 with the Grox.
- An Embassy (+10).
- Gifts (+10 maximum).
- Using Planet Busters on non-Grox worlds near them (up to +50).
- Missions (up to +50 for completion and +5 for accepting).

Any hostile act toward the Grox, such as stealing an artifact or turning an auto-blaster on their ships, ends the attempt.

The reward is the **Dance With The Devil** badge and safe passage to the core. The cost is a **"Allied with Grox" −200** penalty with every other empire, including ones met later, which usually means galaxy-wide war.

### Reaching the core

- Your homeworld assigns the mission after the Grox have been contacted.
- Recommended gear (StrategyWiki and SporeWiki):
  - Interstellar Drive 3–5 and the Wormhole Key
  - Extreme Health and Extreme Energy
  - Many Energy and Repair Mega Packs
  - AOE Repairs
  - An Auto Blaster
  - A Shield
- **The maze.** The core area is a maze. Range shrinks to about 3 pc, and only a few star "bridges" cross the gap into the inner zone (GameRevolution FAQ).
- **Common methods:**
  - **Mad dash** with allied ships soaking up fire. Never stop in a Grox system.
  - **Clear a path** by conquering Grox colonies, which is easy because T0 colonies have few buildings.
  - **Ally the Grox.**
- **Tricks:**
  - Drop a Colony Incredi-Pak on an empty planet as a repair stop.
  - Look for stars above or below the galactic plane.
  - Use wormholes. SporeWiki says 7–8 lie inside the inner circle.

### Steve and the Staff of Life

1. **The voice.** Entering the core plays a cinematic. A deep voice says: "Greetings my friend! You have traveled very far… Join us! Mwahahahaha!! No, just kidding. It's not scary." The tunnel effect evokes *2001: A Space Odyssey*, and llamas line the tunnel walls.
2. **Steve.** A tiny UFO appears, a near-exact copy of the one on the *SimCity 2000* box. Its squeaky pilot announces "I'm Steve! You are now to be given the power… to create and spread life, intelligence and understanding throughout the cosmos." He closes with a timeshare pitch: "Just look for the third rock from Sol." He is the only character who speaks the game's selected language.
3. **The reward.** The player receives the **Staff of Life** (once per save) and the *42* achievement for finding the centre of the galaxy. The Sporepedia history records that the empire "attained enlightenment."

**Staff of Life details:**

- Instantly turns any planet into a **T3 world with a full ecosystem** of random Sporepedia creatures.
- **42 uses**, a *Hitchhiker's Guide* reference. 2-minute cooldown. Charges can never be replenished without mods.
- Using it on a Grox planet wipes out the Grox colonies there.
- It does not count toward Terra-Wrangler or Zoologist.
- Before release it was called the "Genesis Device," after *Star Trek II*. The Galactic Edition handbook misnames it the "Staff of Light."

**Reaching the core is generally treated as "beating" Spore.** The game nevertheless never ends. The player keeps the whole galaxy to conquer, ally, buy or terraform, and the Space Stage was designed to be endless.

## 4.11 Saved games, pollination and the "massively single-player" galaxy

- **Pollination.** *Spore* is "massively single-player." Empires, creatures and buildings are **pollinated** from the online Sporepedia, with priority given to subscribed Sporecasts and buddies. Offline players get Maxis content. Pollinated species do not keep their creator's archetype.
- **Saved games share one galaxy.** Every save lives in the same galaxy, and other saves' homeworlds appear with a **blue ring**, visible from about 50 pc away.
  - Visiting your own other empire works like dealing with an AI of that archetype, but it sells your stock and can't be bought or conquered.
  - Terraforming, sculpting, colouring, colony tools, destructive weapons and superweapons can't be used on its home system.
  - Its other colonies *can* be captured, and that save really loses them.
  - Systems you claim, empires you awaken, and black-hole links all persist across saves.
  - Destroying the Grox removes them for every save.
  - A deleted save's empire becomes an ordinary AI empire.
  - *Identity Crisis* achievement: ally two of your own spacefaring species.
- **Bugs:**
  - An eco-disaster can strike another save's homeworld and can't be fixed from the save that receives the alert.
  - A deleted save's empire keeps broken colours.

## 4.13 Easter eggs: Sol, Earth and others

- **Sol system.** Found at **angle 225.06°, distance 7,295.43 pc**. It contains Mercury, Venus, Earth (with the Moon), Mars (with Olympus Mons, the Tharsis Montes and Valles Marineris), Jupiter (with Ganymede), Saturn (with Titan and rings), Uranus and Neptune. Pluto and the other dwarf planets are missing, and the asteroid belt was added in a patch.
  - Will Wright revealed it by warping to Earth with a console command during a **NASA** demonstration.
  - Sol can't be colonized by AI empires, and Monoliths don't work there.
  - Black-hole locations are fixed in every galaxy. Exiting the black hole nearest the core puts Sol at the edge of Drive-5 SETI range.
- **Earth.** A T1 world with Earth-shaped continents built from USGS topographic maps; Maxis later released it as a *Galactic Adventures* template. Its spice is usually yellow but can be other colours.
  - Finding it earns *Manifest Destiny*. Planet-busting it earns *Oh the Humanity!*
  - Steve's "third rock from Sol" line points to it, but there is no breakfast there.
  - If Earth's geography spawns as the player's crashed-ship planet, a known bug blocks progress.
- **Other secrets:**
  - Storybook planets, crop circles and llama moai rock sculptures.
  - Spinning the main-menu galaxy very fast reveals photos of the producers.
  - SporeWiki's examples of Space-stage nods include Steve's speech echoing Chaplin's *The Great Dictator*, the Monolith (*2001* and *SimEarth*), Spice (*Dune*), and Steve's UFO (*SimCity 2000*).

## 4.15 Patches, bugs and common complaints

**Patch history (SporeWiki):**

- **Patch 1.02** (October 19, 2008): fixed Space-phase water-level and terraform display bugs, the destroy-turrets mission, a Planet Buster timing bug during Grox invasions, and a crash when zooming out from the galactic centre.
- **Patch 1.04** (June 23, 2009): allowed buying more charges per transaction, increased turret range and damage against spacecraft, made all Grox worlds reachable, and fixed the terraform tutorial.
- **Patch 1.05** (July 22, 2009): fixed Space crashes, **reduced disaster rates on all difficulties**, adjusted Hard mode, **increased trade-route limits**, changed Knight tool unlocks, and fixed unreachable Grox planets.

**Recurring complaints:**

- **Constant interruptions.** Eco-disasters, pirate raids, invasions and ally distress calls pull the player back across the map.
- **Spice runs.** Spice must be collected by flying to each colony; Destructoid complained "you've gotta fly to every individual planet with a spice mine on it."
- **Combat.** Ship controls are "stiff and imprecise and combat is a chore" (Destructoid), and the review found the stage "very, very tedious near the end."
- **Fan workarounds.** Players report that pirate harassment persisted after patching and turned to mods and cheats.
- **Exploits.** Save and reload resets superpower cooldowns. Reloading during the terraform-tutorial briefing duplicates free tools.

**Critical reception.** Reviewers generally rated the Space Stage the deepest part of the game. Destructoid called it "brilliantly nonlinear," with "at least three distinctly separate and satisfying methods" for each goal, and noted that it "never really ends." The *New York Times* found the core play dynamics "rather thin."
